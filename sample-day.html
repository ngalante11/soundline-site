A Tuesday

One seventh grader. One day. Hour by hour.

September, the first six-week cycle. The Tuesday we'll run, followed through one student's day. No name, because it isn't any one child's day yet.

The day, as a sounding.

The line marks where the deep work is.

  1. 8:30Arrival

    The door.

    Gina's there. Bag on the hook, phone in the box, a word about the weekend.

  2. 8:45Launch

    Advisory, fifteen minutes.

    Eight to ten students, one guide for three years. Today's goal: clear the proportions gate, draft the lab question.

  3. 9:00Practice · 50 min
    Practice

    Math, at their own gate.

    The gate opens when they can show it to a guide, not when the unit ends. Across the table, a sixth grader is on a gate this student cleared in week one; beside them, an eighth grader is on one they'll reach by winter.

  4. 9:50Practice · 40 min
    Practice

    Reading and writing.

    Today's text: how ice carved the Sound, because the lab needs it. Two paragraphs, one an argument.

  5. 10:30Seminar · 30 min
    Out loud

    The seminar. Today, math.

    Every morning at 10:30 the whole school sits down with one real thing from town and one question, and the students name the skills it takes. Tuesday is math: someone proves why the shortcut works; this student finds the case where it doesn’t. The fourth try lands. Monday is history, Wednesday literature, Thursday science; Friday is All In.

  6. 11:0040 min
    Outside

    Lunch and outside.

    Outside, every day, whatever the weather. Most of what a guide learns about a student, they learn here.

Afternoon

  1. 11:40Lab · 95 min
    Making

    The sounder.

    A sensor on a line that reads the depth of a tank: the sounding line, rebuilt. Today is calibration, and the graph bends where it shouldn't. Defended Friday.

  2. 1:15Studio · 60 min
    Making

    The Quest.

    Week three of six. The client is the children's librarian: an exhibit on how sound travels through water. The group has a plan, forty dollars, and a speaker that won't stay sealed. A guide is in the room and does not fix it. From the second cycle, Tuesday afternoons are apprenticeships for grades 7 and 8.

  3. 2:15Ensemble · 45 min
    Making

    The ensemble.

    Everyone plays something; nobody has to be good yet. The bass part is this student's.

  4. 3:00To 5:30, if you need it
    Extended Day

    Home, or not yet.

    A lesson at 3:15, then the homework desk until 5:30. Or the carline. The quest stays at school.

Imagined. Our first students arrive in September 2027.

How the day adds up.

Practice: two hours.

Ninety minutes at their own gate, thirty out loud. Screens here, for the adaptive work, and nowhere else.

Making: four hours.

Lab, quest, ensemble, and forty minutes outside. Taught by a person, built for someone who asked.

Where the stretch is.

The fourth try at 10:30, and Friday's graph. The guide knows which one is hard.

What goes in the folder.

The gate, if it opened. The paragraph. The graph. By eighth grade the folder is a portfolio; this Tuesday is one page.
