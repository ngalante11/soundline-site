## Key Question 1: Statutory requirements for an SGO under §25F (501(c)(3), 10+ students/more than one school, 90% rule, 300% AMGI, $1,700 cap, carryforward, earmarking)

### Takeaway
Section 25F (added by §70411 of P.L. 119-21, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, 4 July 2025) gives individuals a nonrefundable credit equal to cash gifts to a listed SGO, capped at $1,700 per taxpayer per year, reduced by any state credit for the same gift, with a five-year FIFO carryforward and no §170 deduction for the credited amount. An SGO must be a 501(c)(3) public charity (not a private foundation), keep §25F gifts in a segregated account, serve 10+ students not all at one school, spend at least 90% of its income on scholarships for eligible students (households at or below 300% of area median gross income who are eligible to enroll in a public K-12 school), give priority to prior-year recipients and then siblings, never earmark gifts for a particular student, verify household income, not award to "disqualified persons," and appear on its state's list.

### Cited Findings

**Credit mechanics [STATUTE, as restated by IRS]**
- §25F(a): "in the case of an individual who is a citizen or resident of the United States ... there is allowed as a credit against the tax imposed by chapter 1 ... an amount equal to the aggregate amount of qualified contributions made by the taxpayer during the taxable year." — [IRS Notice 2025-70, §2.02](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)
- §25F(b)(1): the credit "may not exceed $1,700" per taxpayer per taxable year; §25F(b)(2): the credit "is reduced by the amount allowed as a credit on any State tax return of the taxpayer for qualified contributions made by the taxpayer during the taxable year." — [IRS Notice 2025-70, §2.02](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)
- §25F(e): "any qualified contribution for which a § 25F credit is allowed cannot be taken into account as a charitable contribution for purposes of § 170." — [IRS Notice 2025-70, §2.02](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)
- §25F(f): excess credit (over the §26(a) limitation net of credits under §§21, 22, 24, 25, 25A, 25B, 25C, 25E and 26) carries to the succeeding year; "no credit may be carried forward ... to any taxable year following the fifth taxable year after the taxable year in which the credit arose"; credits are "treated as used on a first-in, first-out basis." — [IRS Notice 2025-70, §2.02](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)
- Joint filers: the IRS program page and IR-2026-117 state the credit is "up to $1,700 for individuals, or up to $3,400 for married couples filing jointly." — [IRS FSTC page](https://www.irs.gov/government-entities/federal-state-local-governments/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-fstc); [IR-2026-117, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/the-trump-administration-advances-the-establishment-of-americas-first-nationwide-school-choice-program). (Some earlier practitioner commentary had expected a $1,700 joint cap; the $3,400 figure is Treasury's reading, via search summary of [Current Federal Tax Developments](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2026/10/1/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-regulations-comprehensive-technical-analysis-of-temporary-td-10057-and-proposed-reg-117199-25-under-section-25f).)
- "Qualified contribution" (§25F(c)(3)) = "a charitable contribution of cash to an SGO that uses the contribution to fund scholarships for eligible students ... solely within the State in which the organization is listed pursuant to § 25F(g)." — [Rev. Proc. 2026-6, §2.01](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-26-06.pdf)
- Effective date: "Section 25F applies to taxable years ending after Dec. 31, 2026"; credit launches 1 Jan 2027. — [IR-2026-117](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/the-trump-administration-advances-the-establishment-of-americas-first-nationwide-school-choice-program)
- Donors may contribute to an SGO in any participating state regardless of where the donor lives. — [IR-2026-117](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/the-trump-administration-advances-the-establishment-of-americas-first-nationwide-school-choice-program)
- No aggregate national cap or sunset date appears in any IRS document reviewed (Notice 2025-70, Rev. Proc. 2026-6, IR-2026-117); searches for a cap or sunset returned nothing. — [IRS Notice 2025-70](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)

**SGO definition, §25F(c)(5) [STATUTE, as restated by IRS]**
An organization qualifies only if it:
- "(a) be described in § 501(c)(3), be exempt from tax under § 501(a), and not be a private foundation; (b) prevent the co-mingling of qualified contributions with other amounts by maintaining one or more separate accounts exclusively for qualified contributions; (c) satisfy each of the requirements of § 25F(d); and (d) be included on the list submitted for the applicable covered State under § 25F(g) for the applicable year." — [IRS Notice 2025-70, §2.03(1)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)

**Operating requirements, §25F(d) [STATUTE, as restated by IRS]**
- "(a) The organization must provide scholarships to 10 or more students who do not all attend the same school."
- "(b) The organization cannot spend less than 90 percent of its income on scholarships for eligible students."
- "(c) The organization cannot provide scholarships for any expenses other than qualified elementary or secondary education expenses" (defined by cross-reference to §530(b)(3)(A), Coverdell ESAs).
- "(d) The organization must provide scholarships to eligible students with a priority for: (i) students awarded a scholarship the previous school year, and thereafter, and (ii) any eligible students who have a sibling who was awarded a scholarship from such organization."
- "(e) The organization cannot earmark or set aside contributions for scholarships on behalf of any particular student."
- "(f) The organization must: (i) verify the annual household income and family size of eligible students who apply for scholarships to ensure such students meet the area median gross income requirement of § 25F(c)(2)(A), and (ii) limit the awarding of scholarships to eligible students who are members of a household for which the income does not exceed [that amount]."
- "(g) The organization cannot award a scholarship to any disqualified person, which § 25F(d)(2)(B) provides is determined pursuant to rules similar to the rules of § 4946 (relating to private foundations)." — all from [IRS Notice 2025-70, §2.03(2)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)

**Eligible student, §25F(c)(2) [STATUTE]**
- "an individual who is a member of a household with an income that, for the calendar year prior to the date of the application for a scholarship, is not greater than 300 percent of the area median gross income (as such term is used in § 42)." — [IRS Notice 2025-70, §4.05(3)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)
- The second prong is that the student "is eligible to enroll in a public elementary or secondary school." — via search summary of [26 USC 25F (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/25F) and [CRS R48724](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48724)

**90% rule interpretation [IRS GUIDANCE / PROP REG]**
- Treasury anticipated in Notice 2025-70 that "the income of the organization includes all income of the organization, including unrelated business income, and is not limited to qualified contributions segregated in the separate account(s)." — [IRS Notice 2025-70, §4.01](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)
- Treasury's June 2026 preview and the October 2026 regulations added a safe harbor: for SGOs primarily engaged in scholarship granting, the 90% test can be measured against the §25F segregated account (contributions plus earnings) rather than total receipts; multistate SGOs must satisfy it separately for each state account. — via search summaries of [KPMG, June 2026](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/06/treasury-previews-sgo-tax-credits.html) and [Treasury Preview PDF](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/136/Preview-of-Forthcoming-Guidance.pdf)
- IR-2026-117 states: "Multistate SGOs whose activities are at least 85% scholarship-granting may qualify for an operational safe harbor." — [IR-2026-117](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/the-trump-administration-advances-the-establishment-of-americas-first-nationwide-school-choice-program)
- Under the proposed rules, 90% compliance "is measured as of the last day of the taxable year following the taxable year in which such income is received." — via search summary of [Federal Register, REG-117199-25](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/02/2026-20277/federal-scholarship-tax-credit)

**Earmarking: student vs. school**
- Statute bars earmarking "on behalf of any particular student." Donors may choose which SGO to support; multistate SGOs must let donors "designate how their qualified contribution should be allocated among one or more of the covered States" on whose lists the SGO appears. — [IRS Notice 2025-70, §§2.03(2)(e), 3.03(5)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf); via search summary of [Federal Register, REG-117199-25](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/02/2026-20277/federal-scholarship-tax-credit)
- School-level designation by donors is NOT addressed by the statute or the regulations; commentators disagree. One law-firm analysis says "the Regulations do not address whether school-specific donor designations are permitted"; a vendor guide reads the silence as permissive provided the SGO makes every award decision; another compliance guide warns the ban reaches "structural arrangements that functionally create earmarking, such as allocating scholarship funds by school in proportion to donations from that school's community." — via search summaries of [Brown & Streza](https://www.brownandstreza.com/the-new-sgo-rulebook-what-code-25f-and-the-treasury-regulations-require-of-scholarship-granting-organizations-part-1/), [Clark Nuber](https://clarknuber.com/articles/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-section-25f-2027/), [sgoguide.com](https://sgoguide.com/blog/compliant-scholarship-award-process)
- Agudath Israel reported that a Jewish-community SGO "intend[s] to let donors earmark their contributions for specific school or for a specific community." — via search summary of [Teach Coalition](https://teachcoalition.org/federalscholarships/)
- Notice 2025-70 says Treasury does "not anticipate that the forthcoming proposed regulations would prohibit an SGO from itself imposing additional governing provisions beyond the requirements imposed by § 25F(c)(5) unless such a provision would conflict with the ability of the SGO to satisfy such requirements." — [IRS Notice 2025-70, §3.02](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)

**Disqualified persons [IRS GUIDANCE]**
- Treasury is considering defining "substantial contributor" for §25F as anyone who gave more than 2% of the SGO's cumulative contributions, and expects selection-committee members and their immediate families to be disqualified persons. — [IRS Notice 2025-70, §4.04](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)

**SGO reporting, registration and donor substantiation [TEMP REG / PROP REG, via search summaries]**
- SGOs must register electronically in the IRS "FSTC SGO Portal" and use "unique donor numbers rather than collecting donors' Social Security numbers," provide donor acknowledgements and reports of qualified contributions, "submit annual reports and undergo audits." — [IR-2026-117](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/the-trump-administration-advances-the-establishment-of-americas-first-nationwide-school-choice-program); [IRS FSTC page](https://www.irs.gov/government-entities/federal-state-local-governments/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-fstc)
- Registration data: name, EIN, address, phone, year of formation, contact person, taxable year; registration is to be completed before 1 Jan 2027. Donor written acknowledgments with donor number due by 31 January following each calendar year; SGO contribution reports to the IRS through the portal by 28 February. — via search summaries of [Dykema](https://www.dykema.com/news-insights/treasury-releases-section-25f-scholarship-tax-credit-regulations.html), [eftccredit.com T.D. 10057 summary](https://eftccredit.com/documents/treasury-temporary-regulations-td-10057), [Current Federal Tax Developments](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2026/10/1/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-regulations-comprehensive-technical-analysis-of-temporary-td-10057-and-proposed-reg-117199-25-under-section-25f)
- Donors claim the credit on new Form 8525, "Federal Scholarship Tax Credit," listing the SGO and donor number; donors must tell the SGO the payment is a qualified §25F contribution. — [IRS FSTC page](https://www.irs.gov/government-entities/federal-state-local-governments/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-fstc)
- The proposed rules reportedly attach an annual §25F certification to the SGO's Form 990 and copy it to each listing state; this is a PROPOSED item only. — via search summary of [Current Federal Tax Developments](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2026/10/1/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-regulations-comprehensive-technical-analysis-of-temporary-td-10057-and-proposed-reg-117199-25-under-section-25f)
- A Paperwork Reduction Act notice on the SGO information collection was published 21 Oct 2026 (91 FR 66621). — via search summary of [Federal Register, 2026-21477](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/21/2026-21477/agency-information-collection-activities-requesting-comment-on-qualified-contributions-to-a)

**Donor protection if an SGO is later disqualified [IRS GUIDANCE]**
- If the organization was on the state list at the time of the gift, "the donor would generally be treated as having made a contribution to an SGO," unless the donor "was aware of, or was responsible to any extent for" the deficiencies. — [IRS Notice 2025-70, §3.02](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)

### Inferences
- A brand-new SGO can satisfy "10 or more students who do not all attend the same school" only once it is actually granting scholarships; the state certification (see Q3) is phrased in the present tense ("provides scholarships to ten (10) or more students"), so a start-up SGO should expect to show at minimum a credible, documented award plan, and should ask the state whether a first-year organization can be certified on the basis of organizational documents and planned operations (Notice 2025-70 §3.03(6) refers to organizational documents or bylaws that "require" compliance plus operation "in a manner that satisfies" the rules).
- The 90% rule, measured as of the end of the following taxable year under the proposed regs, gives a new SGO roughly a two-year window to deploy first-year receipts, but start-up costs still count against the 10% (Treasury explicitly asked for comments on start-up fluctuations and has not, in the documents read, granted relief).
- The absence of any school-designation rule is the single biggest open design question for a school-affiliated SGO; the safest reading is that donor "preferences" for a school are tolerable only if the SGO retains genuine, documented discretion over awards and does not run per-school sub-accounts that mirror donations.

## Key Question 2: What counts as an eligible "elementary or secondary school" and "qualified elementary or secondary education expenses"? Do new/unaccredited schools qualify?

### Takeaway
§25F borrows the Coverdell definitions: qualified expenses are those in §530(b)(3)(A) (tuition, fees, academic tutoring, special-needs services, books, supplies, equipment and similar items connected with enrollment or attendance at a public, private or religious K-12 school), and "school" is "any school that provides elementary or secondary education (kindergarten through grade 12), as determined under State law." No federal accreditation or state-recognition requirement appears in the statute or regulations, and states are expressly barred from restricting the type of school recipients may attend; whether a not-yet-open school is a "school" therefore turns on New York law, which no source addressed.

### Cited Findings
- §25F(c)(4) "defines a qualified elementary or secondary education expense as any expense described in § 530(b)(3)(A) (relating to Coverdell education savings accounts) of an eligible student. Section 530(b)(3)(A) identifies these expenses to include certain expenses incurred at, required by, or provided by a public, private, or religious school." — [IRS Notice 2025-70, §2.03(2)(c)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf) [STATUTE]
- IR-2026-117 lists scholarship uses: "private-school tuition, academic tutoring, special-needs services, books, supplies, computers and other equipment, and other expenses tied to enrollment or attendance." — [IR-2026-117](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/the-trump-administration-advances-the-establishment-of-americas-first-nationwide-school-choice-program) [IRS GUIDANCE]
- §530(b)(3)(B): "the term 'school' means any school that provides elementary or secondary education (kindergarten through grade 12), as determined under State law"; Treasury's proposed rule adopts this cross-reference and would recognize public, private and religious K-12 schools, homeschools where state law treats them as schools, and tribal schools. — via search summaries of [Scholarship Tax Credits](https://www.scholarshiptaxcredits.org/section-25f-education-freedom-tax-credit/), [AFC Scholarship Fund](https://afcscholarshipfund.org/articles/eftc-homeschool-school-definition-state-law/), [KPMG June 2026](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/06/treasury-previews-sgo-tax-credits.html) [PROP REG]
- An expense qualifies only if it satisfies §530(b)(3)(A) "including the applicable connection to a school described in section 530(b)(3)(B)." — via search summary of [Baker & Hostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/tax-credit-for-donations-to-scholarship-granting-organizations/)
- States "may not impose SGO operating requirements stricter than section 25F, including limits based on school type or allowable expenses" and "may not use discretionary certification standards to exclude otherwise qualifying SGOs." — [IR-2026-117](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/the-trump-administration-advances-the-establishment-of-americas-first-nationwide-school-choice-program) [TEMP REG / PROP REG]
- Commentary: the program "does not include any additional requirements related to a student's receipt of a scholarship, nor does it place any requirements on private schools that enroll scholarship recipients." — via search summary of [CRS R48724](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48724)
- Detailed guidance on the §530 expense list is a separate, still-outstanding workstream; the October rules "left out detailed guidance on what counts as eligible expenses, and the IRS said more would come without a set timeline." — via search summaries of [Chalkbeat Detroit, 27 Oct 2026](https://www.chalkbeat.org/detroit/2026/10/27/michigan-governor-gretchen-whitmer-raises-questions-about-tax-credit-program/) and [AFC Scholarship Fund](https://afcscholarshipfund.org/articles/treasury-25f-rules-preview-eftc-donors-sgos/)
- Public school students are eligible: scholarships "can be used for private school tuition or for public school expenses such as afterschool programs, technology supplies or tutoring." — via search summary of [Chalkbeat NY, 16 Sep 2026](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2026/09/16/hochul-federal-school-choice-tax-credit-unions-opt-out/) [PRESS]
- Eligible-student test is eligibility to enroll in a public school, so homeschooled children can be eligible students even where homeschool costs may not be qualified expenses. — via search summary of [sgoguide.com](https://sgoguide.com/blog/can-homeschoolers-use-sgos) [ADVOCATE]

### Inferences
- Because "school" is defined by state law and the federal rules forbid state-added school-type restrictions, a new New York private school's eligibility depends on whether it is a nonpublic school under New York Education Law at the time scholarship funds are spent (i.e., when a student is enrolled and tuition is incurred), not on accreditation. A school that has not yet opened cannot have "enrollment or attendance" expenses, so scholarships cannot be disbursed to it until it is operating; nothing found prevents an SGO from listing a planned school as a future partner.
- The SGO, not the school, is the regulated entity; a school's practical obligations are to be a "school" under NY law, to invoice qualified expenses, and to cooperate with the SGO's documentation.

## Key Question 3: Treasury/IRS guidance issued 2025-2026 and what it says about state elections, SGO lists and timing

### Takeaway
Five instruments exist: Notice 2025-70 (8 Dec 2025 IRB; comments closed 26 Dec 2025), Rev. Proc. 2026-6 with Form 15714 (12 Dec 2025, IR-2025-121), a Treasury "Preview of Forthcoming Guidance" (c. 9-10 June 2026), and on 1-2 Oct 2026 temporary regulations T.D. 10057 (state elections, lists, SGO registration) plus proposed regulations REG-117199-25 (substantive SGO/credit rules; comments due 1 Dec 2026, hearing 15 Dec 2026). For 2027, a first-year state must file Form 15714 by 1 Jan 2027 and submit its certified SGO list via the IRS State portal by 15 Feb 2027, or no SGO in that state qualifies for 2027.

### Cited Findings

**Notice 2025-70 (Dec 2025) [IRS GUIDANCE]**
- Published at 2025-50 I.R.B. 773 (8 Dec 2025); requested comments by 26 Dec 2025 (docket IRS-2025-0466). — [Rev. Proc. 2026-6 §2.02](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-26-06.pdf); [Notice 2025-70 §5](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)
- Statutory timing: "Not later than January 1 of each calendar year (or, with respect to the 2027 calendar year, as early as practicable), a State that voluntarily elects to participate under § 25F must provide to the Secretary ... a list of the SGOs that meet the requirements described in § 25F(c)(5) and are located in the State." — [Notice 2025-70 §2.04(1)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf) [STATUTE]
- Who elects: "The election under § 25F(g) must be made by the Governor of the State or by such other individual, agency, or entity as is designated under State law to make such elections on behalf of the State with respect to Federal tax benefits." — [Notice 2025-70 §2.04(2)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf) [STATUTE]
- Treasury interprets §25F(g) "as requiring each covered State to verify that each organization on the State's list satisfies all of the requirements of § 25F(c)(5). ... Reliance by a covered State on self-certifications by SGOs would not be sufficient." — [Notice 2025-70 §3.03](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)
- Anticipated annual certification by the state official, under penalties of perjury, of: each SGO's name/EIN/address/phone and single-state vs multistate status; 501(c)(3) public-charity status; segregated accounts; for single-state SGOs, that each "Provides scholarships to ten (10) or more students in that State who do not all attend the same school," spends 90%+ on scholarships, limits to qualified expenses, selects only in-state eligible students with the statutory priorities, does not earmark for a particular student, and does not award to disqualified persons; for multistate SGOs, that they require donor state designation and track state-designated funds; that "the State has adopted, and is complying with, policies and procedures designed to enable the State to make its own independent determination" of compliance (checking the IRS EO BMF extract suffices for 501(c)(3) status); that the state "will promptly notify the IRS" of removals; description of any state tax credit; and authority to act. — [Notice 2025-70 §3.03(1)-(9)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)
- "the State list must include all organizations located in the State that have requested to be designated as an SGO and that meet the § 25F(c)(5) statutory requirements." — [Notice 2025-70 §3.02](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)
- Treasury anticipated requiring electronic submission of election, list and certification. — [Notice 2025-70 §3.02](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)

**Rev. Proc. 2026-6 and Form 15714 (12 Dec 2025) [IRS GUIDANCE]**
- IR-2025-121 (12 Dec 2025) announced Rev. Proc. 2026-6, "the exclusive procedure for a State to make an election to be a 'covered state' prior to identifying scholarship granting organizations" (an "Advance Election") for 2027. — [IR-2025-121](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/treasury-irs-allow-states-to-make-an-advance-election-to-participate-in-the-new-federal-tax-credit-for-individual-contributions-to-scholarship-granting-organizations-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill); [Rev. Proc. 2026-6 §1](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-26-06.pdf)
- Form 15714 may be filed "on or after January 1, 2026, and before the final date on which the State is permitted to submit the State SGO list"; "No alternative method ... and no alteration of Form 15714, will be accepted"; any SGO list attached "will not be processed"; the IRS acknowledges receipt by email. — [Rev. Proc. 2026-6 §§4.01-4.03](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-26-06.pdf)
- Consequence of missing the list deadline: "a failure to submit the list by the deadline would not meet the statutory requirements, and, as a result, no organization in that State would qualify as an SGO under § 25F for calendar year 2027." — [Rev. Proc. 2026-6 §3.02](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-26-06.pdf)
- Form 15714 Part II: "For the period beginning January 1, 2027, and ending December 31, 2027, the above State elects to participate under section 25F." Part IV requires the signer to certify being "The Governor of the above State" or "The individual designated under State law to make such elections with respect to Federal tax credits" or the authorized representative of an agency so designated; a non-governor signer "must include ... the enacted statutory and/or regulatory provisions that are binding on the above State and therefore establish the authority." Filed by email to 25FAdvanceElection@irs.gov or fax 833-312-3360.
