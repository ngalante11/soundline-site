## Key Question 1: What do SGOs (and §25F) require from a school before listing it, and what does an SGO participation agreement typically contain?

### Takeaway
§25F itself imposes no school-registration, accreditation or "listing" requirement on schools; its requirements run to the SGO (10+ students not all at one school, 90% payout, income verification, no earmarking for a student) and expenses must be ones "incurred at, required by, or provided by a public, private, or religious school" under §530(b)(3)(A). The school-facing gatekeeping comes from state law in existing programs (Illinois: ISBE recognition/registration; Florida: 3 years of operation or a surety bond; Georgia: accreditation-in-process, Civil Rights Act compliance) and from each SGO's own agreement (enrollment verification, fee schedules, tuition caps, refund on withdrawal).

### Cited Findings
- **[STATUTE]** §25F(d) SGO requirements as restated by the IRS: "(a) The organization must provide scholarships to 10 or more students who do not all attend the same school. (b) The organization cannot spend less than 90 percent of its income on scholarships for eligible students. (c) The organization cannot provide scholarships for any expenses other than qualified elementary or secondary education expenses … Section 530(b)(3)(A) identifies these expenses to include certain expenses incurred at, required by, or provided by a public, private, or religious school. (d) … priority for (i) students awarded a scholarship the previous school year … (ii) any eligible students who have a sibling who was awarded a scholarship … (e) The organization cannot earmark or set aside contributions for scholarships on behalf of any particular student. (f) The organization must (i) verify the annual household income and family size of eligible students … (g) The organization cannot award a scholarship to any disqualified person" — [IRS Notice 2025-70, pp. 4–5](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)
- **[STATUTE]** "Eligible student" = "an individual who is a member of a household with an income that, for the calendar year prior to the date of the application for a scholarship, is not greater than 300 percent of the area median gross income (as such term is used in § 42)" — [IRS Notice 2025-70, p. 18](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)
- **[IRS GUIDANCE]** What states must certify for each listed SGO: 501(c)(3) public charity (not private foundation); "located in the state"; accounts for scholarship funds and uses a segregated account; complies with scholarship eligibility requirements. An organization "must ask to be included on a participating state's SGO list and provide the information the state needs." No school-level certification is listed — [IRS, Federal Scholarship Tax Credit (FSTC) page](https://www.irs.gov/government-entities/federal-state-local-governments/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-fstc)
- **[PROP REG, via secondary]** Treasury's June 2026 preview said "school" will follow §530, covering public, private and religious K-12 schools "as defined by state law"; the proposed regs do not separately address home schools, microschools or tribal schools — [AESA summary of Treasury preview](https://www.aesa.us/2026/06/15/treasury-outlines-sgo-parameters-expected-in-regulations/); [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/proposed-federal-school-choice-regulations-what-private-schools-need-to-know)
- **[PROP REG, via secondary]** Proposed §1.25F-3(c)(1) requires an SGO "to provide scholarships to 10 or more students who do not all attend the same school" — [Baker & Hostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/tax-credit-for-donations-to-scholarship-granting-organizations/)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – Illinois]** Under the (now-expired) Invest in Kids Act a "qualified school" had to be "a non-public school located in Illinois and recognized by the Board pursuant to Section 2-3.25o of the School Code"; the Illinois Dept. of Revenue page says a school must be "recognized by the Board or registered with the Board and awaiting approval of recognition," and "if the school does not become recognized by the Board, the SGO cannot release scholarship funds for a student attending it." The program expired 31 Dec 2023 — [Illinois DOR, Invest in Kids – Schools](https://tax.illinois.gov/programs/investinkids/schools.html); [ISBE evaluation report](https://www.isbe.net/Documents/Invest-in-Kids-Act-Evaluation-Report.pdf)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – Illinois]** Before the 2018 fix, the recognition-only rule "prevented 257 registered schools … from participating" — [Agudath Israel of Illinois](https://agudah.org/agudath-israel-of-illinois-lauds-bi-partisan-solution-to-scholarship-tax-credit-program-impasse)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – Florida]** Fla. Stat. 1002.421 fiscal-soundness test for private schools accepting scholarship students: "Being in operation for at least 3 school years or obtaining a surety bond or letter of credit for the amount equal to the scholarship funds for any quarter and filing the surety bond or letter of credit with the department" — [Florida Senate, s. 1002.421 (2025)](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2025/1002.421); [Step Up For Students accountability paper](https://www.stepupforstudents.org/wp-content/uploads/22.12-Scholarship-Accountability.pdf)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – Florida]** Step Up For Students' 2026-27 Private School Provider Handbook requires schools to send "proof of admission, the school's and student's fee schedules, and any other information needed to process payments"; "students using the private school option must confirm enrollment at an eligible private school in EMA" to receive funding — [Step Up Private School Provider Handbook](https://www.stepupforstudents.org/?p=67789); [Private School Handbook PDF](https://go.stepupforstudents.org/hubfs/HANDBOOKS/School%20and%20Provider%20Handbooks/Private%20School%20Handbook.pdf)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – Georgia]** Eligible schools must be "accredited or in the process of becoming accredited" by a listed agency, "located in Georgia," "adhere to the provision of the federal Civil Rights Act of 1964," and satisfy Georgia private-school law — [AAA Scholarships Georgia school eligibility flyer](https://www.aaascholarships.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Georgia-School-Eligibility-Flyer-1.pdf); [Georgia tax-credit scholarships overview, June 2025](https://foropportunity.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Georgia-Tax-Credit-Scholarships-Web-June-2025.pdf)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – Georgia GOAL]** "When GOAL approves a scholarship recommendation, a GOAL Scholarship Agreement is sent to school officials for execution by the parents, an authorized school official, and GOAL"; "Each school makes the final determination for the scholarship award amount to submit to GOAL for approval, based on family finances and available funds"; it is "up to each GOAL participating school to design its own financial aid and GOAL Scholarship application process" — [Tallulah Falls School – Georgia GOAL](https://www.tallulahfalls.org/admissions/tuition-affordability/georgia-goal-scholarship); [LaGrange Academy – GOAL](https://www.lagrangeacademy.org/support-us/goal-program.cfm); if a recipient withdraws mid-year, "the funds returned to GOAL are prorated" — [Mount Paran Christian School handbook](https://handbooks.mtparanschool.com/books/2026-2027-mount-paran-christian-school-parent-and-student-handbook/page/goal-student-scholarship-procedures)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – Indiana]** "An SGO may not limit the availability of scholarships to students of only one participating school (IC 20-51-3-5)"; a donor "may designate a participating school for which the contribution must be used as scholarships under IC 20-51-3-3" — [Indiana DOE SGO FAQ for Parents and Donors](https://secure.in.gov/tos/inesa/files/SGO-FAQ-for-Parents-and-Donors.pdf); IDOE publishes an annual list pairing each participating school with its SGO(s) — [2025 SGO Participating Schools](https://secure.in.gov/doe/students/files/2025-SGO-Participating-Schools.pdf)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – Arizona]** ADOR certifies STOs; an STO "must not award scholarships to students who are simultaneously enrolled in a district school or charter school and a qualified school" — [Arizona DOR, Certification for School Tuition Organizations](https://azdor.gov/node/176); an example of an STO's written school agreement exists (Pappas Kids 2026-2027 School Agreement) but could not be read in this session — [Pappas Kids School Agreement](https://pappaskidssf.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-2027-School-Agreement-Pappas-Kids.pdf)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – general]** Illinois' administrative code for SGO scholarship issuance (86 Ill. Adm. Code 1000.600) is a model for state-level issuance rules — [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/illinois/Ill-Admin-Code-tit-86-SS-1000.600)
- **[IRS GUIDANCE]** The §25F credit is not a federal grant to the school: one firm notes "participation in the scholarship program will not subject independent schools to federal compliance obligations as a recipient of federal financial assistance," though it "remains to be seen whether … regulations … will impose any additional requirements on independent schools" — [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/08/federal-school-choice-program-2025-what-it-means)

### Inferences
- For New York there is no state statute (yet) listing "qualified schools" the way Illinois/Florida/Georgia do; absent a New York implementing statute, the school-level test will be whatever each New York SGO writes into its participation agreement plus the federal §530 "school … as determined under State law" concept. In New York the practical proxy for "a school under state law" is a NYSED-issued BEDS code for a registered nonpublic school (see KQ2).
- Expect any New York SGO agreement to borrow the common denominators seen above: (1) proof the school is a bona fide nonpublic school (BEDS code, certificate of occupancy, fire-safety report), (2) a nondiscrimination/Civil Rights Act attestation, (3) a published tuition and fee schedule, (4) enrollment verification per payment period, (5) prorated refund on withdrawal, (6) the school's own financial-aid process feeding award recommendations to the SGO, and (7) a limit that awards cannot exceed tuition + fees.
- Florida's "3 years or a surety bond" rule is the most direct precedent for a zero-history school: a New York SGO may ask a brand-new school for a bond, letter of credit, or escrow-style terms before it will pay a quarter's tuition in advance.

### Gaps
- Could not read the actual text of the IQE (Indiana) school agreement, the Empower Illinois school registration page, the OCEN (Ohio) school enrollment form, the Step Up handbook's full school-eligibility section, or the GOAL participating-school agreement (hosts blocked). Their existence is confirmed but exact clauses are not.
- No New York SGO has yet published a school participation agreement for the federal program (none found as of 29 Oct 2026).

## Key Question 2: How does New York recognize a nonpublic school (NYSED registration, BEDS code, substantial equivalency), and can a school get a BEDS code before it has students?

### Takeaway
New York does not license private K-12 schools; the operative "registration" is a BEDS code issued by NYSED's Office of Religious and Independent Schools (ORISS) on an initial application that requires a certificate of occupancy and a local-code-official fire-safety report for the specific site. Applications approved July–December take effect 1 July of that school year, and the published cut-off for a code "to take effect for 2026-27" is mid-December 2026; a school opening September 2027 would be applying for a 2027-28 code. Substantial-equivalency review of a new school occurs within its first two years of instruction, so it is not a pre-opening gate.

### Cited Findings
- **[NY]** ORISS "assigns a separate BEDS code to each site, and the process starts with an initial application for each one"; to keep a code a school "must file the annual BEDS report, keep its building safe and authorized for school use, and notify ORISS of changes" — [NYSED, BEDS Code Information](https://www.nysed.gov/nonpublic-schools/basic-education-data-system-beds-code-information)
- **[NY]** Initial BEDS application (rev. 23 Jan 2026): submit "to ORISS by email at ORISS@nysed.gov, with the subject line 'BEDS Initial application – Applicant School Name'"; requires "a current Certificate of Occupancy or Certificate of Compliance for the site(s)"; "Schools outside of New York City must have a local code enforcement official complete the attached Nonpublic School Building Fire Safety Report"; "BEDS code applications approved between July and December will receive a BEDS code effective July 1 of the same school year"; applicants in October–December must also "submit a completed BEDS Report of Religious and Independent Schools (Nonpublic)" (the enrollment report) — [NYSED BEDS Code Initial Application, rev. 01-23-2026 (.docx)](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/nonpublic-schools/beds-code-initial-application-rev-01-23-2026.docx); earlier version — [Nonpublic School BEDS Initial Application 2017 (PDF)](https://www.p12.nysed.gov/nonpub/documents/Nonpublic_School_BEDS_Initial_Application_2017.pdf)
- **[NY]** "For a code to take effect for 2026-2027, the complete application has to reach NYSED by mid-December"; a school that relocates to a different district needs a new code — [NYSED, Dates to Remember 2026-2027](https://www.nysed.gov/nonpublic-schools/dates-remember-2026-2027)
- **[NY]** Where a certificate of occupancy "does not apply, you may need to obtain a Certificate of No Objection, Certificate of Use, Certificate of Compliance, or equivalent document" — [NYSED 21-item checklist (incorporation)](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/nonpublic-schools/21-item-checklist.pdf)
- **[NY]** The initial fire-safety form "is used only for the initial year; later years use the annual fire safety report" under Education Law §807-a — [NYSED initial fire inspection report form (Jan 2021)](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/nonpublic-schools/initial-fire-inspection-report-form-updated-january-2021.docx)
- **[NY]** NYSED maintains a "Persons Interested in Starting a New School" page (old; roughly 15 years) and a "Running a School" page — [NYSED nonpublic – starting a new school](https://www.p12.nysed.gov/nonpub/personsinterestedinstartinganewschool); [Running a School](https://www.p12.nysed.gov/nonpub/running/)
- **[NY]** The separate BEDS Institutional Master File (IMF) data collection for existing schools is due 20 Nov 2026 — [NYSED IRS, BEDS IMF](https://www.nysed.gov/information-reporting-services/beds-basic-educational-data-system-imf-institutional-master-file); support portal — [BEDS/VADIR support, Religious & Independent Schools](https://bedsvadirsupport.nysed.gov/hc/en-us/sections/203361886-Nonpublic-Schools)
- **[NY]** Substantial equivalency (8 NYCRR Part 130): "For a new nonpublic school, the review must occur within the first 2 years of when the nonpublic school commences instruction and every 7 years thereafter" (law-firm summary of the 2022 final rule) — [Bond Schoeneck & King](https://www.bsk.com/news-events-videos/new-york-state-education-law-regarding-substantial-equivalency-is-modified-by-budget-legislation/print); NYCLU's 2022 comment likewise describes "review of new nonpublic schools within two years of commencing instruction (§130.4(a))" — [NYCLU comment](https://www.nyclu.org/uploads/2022/06/220531-comment-substantiallyequivalentinstruction.pdf); regulation text — [8 NYCRR 130.4 (Justia)](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-york/title-8/chapter-ii/subchapter-f/part-130/section-130-4/)
- **[NY]** Part 130 offers "several 'pathways' through which a nonpublic school may demonstrate substantial equivalency (§130.3)"; the 2025 state budget amended Education Law §3204(6) and NYSED issued statements in May and December 2025 on expanded assessment pathways — [NYSED May 2025 statement](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/nonpublic-schools/2025-05-20-nysed-statement-regarding-2025-budget-amendments-to-the-compulsory-education-law.pdf); [NYSED Dec 2025 second statement](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/nonpublic-schools/2025-12-01-education-law-32046-second-statement-and-further-interim-directions.pdf); the Court of Appeals ruled on Part 130 in June 2025 — [NY Court of Appeals decision](https://www.nycourts.gov/CTAPPs/Decisions/2025/Jun25/56opn25-Decision.pdf)
- **[STATUTE]** Federal expense definition points to §530(b)(3)(A) (expenses "incurred at, required by, or provided by a public, private, or religious school") — [IRS Notice 2025-70, p. 4](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf); Treasury's preview expected "school" to be read per §530 "as defined by state law" — [AESA](https://www.aesa.us/2026/06/15/treasury-outlines-sgo-parameters-expected-in-regulations/)

### Inferences
- A school opening September 2027 should plan to file its initial BEDS application as soon as it has (a) a signed lease/deed for the Miller Place site, (b) a certificate of occupancy for educational use (Town of Brookhaven building department), and (c) the Nonpublic School Building Fire Safety Report completed by the Brookhaven fire marshal/code official. Because approvals in July–December take effect 1 July of that school year, an application approved in July–September 2027 would carry a BEDS code effective 1 July 2027, i.e. before the first day of class. An application filed earlier (spring 2027) is prudent so the code exists when SGOs vet the school in summer 2027; whether ORISS will approve a code with zero enrolled students at filing is not documented (see Gaps), but the form's requirement for an enrollment report only for October–December applicants suggests spring/summer applicants are not asked for enrollment data.
- Substantial equivalency is a post-opening review (within two years of commencing instruction), so it should not block SGO listing in 2027, but an SGO may ask for an attestation that the school will comply with Education Law §3204 and Part 130.
- Because neither §25F nor the temp regs require a state-issued credential for the school, the BEDS code is a *practical* rather than a *legal* prerequisite — it is what a New York SGO will most likely use to verify that the school is a "school … as determined under State law."

### Gaps
- NYSED's pages could not be fetched directly (TLS failure), so the exact 2027-28 application deadline, processing time, and whether ORISS issues a code before any students are enrolled are unconfirmed. The user should confirm with ORISS (ORISS@nysed.gov) whether a pre-opening application is accepted and what effective date it would carry.
- The current text of 8 NYCRR 130.4 for new schools (two-year window) is cited through secondary sources, not the regulation itself.

## Key Question 3: Does §25F allow donors to designate a school (not a student)? How do SGOs handle school-designated funds and fees?

### Takeaway
The statute bars earmarking for a *particular student* and the temp regs require multistate SGOs to let donors designate a *state*; neither the statute nor the proposed regs address school-level designation, and practitioners split (Fisher Phillips: unresolved; Baker & Hostetler: "clearly yes"). Indiana and Ohio practice shows school-designated giving is routine in state programs, with the SGO retaining a fee (Indiana cap 10%; OCEN 9%) and the remainder flowing to scholarships at the designated school.

### Cited Findings
- **[STATUTE]** "The organization cannot earmark or set aside contributions for scholarships on behalf of any particular student" (§25F(d)) — [IRS Notice 2025-70](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)
- **[IRS GUIDANCE / TEMP REG]** A multistate SGO must "require donors to designate the State, on whose State list the organization is named, in which their qualified contribution is to be used" and "track and match qualifying contributions that are designated by the donor to be spent within the State with scholarships to eligible students within the State" — [IRS Notice 2025-70, pp. 10–11](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf); the proposed regs "require a multistate SGO to allow donors to designate how their qualified contribution should be allocated among the covered States" — [Dykema](https://www.dykema.com/news-insights/treasury-releases-section-25f-scholarship-tax-credit-regulations.html)
- **[PROP REG, via secondary]** "The Regulations do not address whether school-specific donor designations are permitted for purposes of the EFTC, and future IRS guidance may be needed to resolve the question" — [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/proposed-federal-school-choice-regulations-what-private-schools-need-to-know); contrasting practitioner view that the answer is "clearly yes" — [Baker & Hostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/tax-credit-for-donations-to-scholarship-granting-organizations/)
- **[PROP REG, via secondary]** "A Section 25F contribution may not be earmarked or set aside for scholarships on behalf of any particular student"; "the donor picks the organization, not the student" — [Baker & Hostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/tax-credit-for-donations-to-scholarship-granting-organizations/)
- **[IRS GUIDANCE]** NAIS framing: "donors cannot mandate that their contribution go to a particular family" — [NAIS Washington Watch](https://www.nais.org/learn/washington-watch)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – Indiana]** Indiana law requires that "a donor be permitted to designate a specific participating school at the time of his or her donation" (a state rule, not §25F) — [Baker & Hostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/tax-credit-for-donations-to-scholarship-granting-organizations/); IC 20-51-3-3 certification agreements require SGOs to "use not more than ten percent (10%) of the total amount of contributions for administrative costs" — [Ind. Code §20-51-3-3 (Justia 2015 text)](https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/2015/title-20/article-51/chapter-3/); [Counsel Stack listing 2026](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/in/20-51-3-3); the IDOE SGO application includes an assurance to "distribute one hundred percent (100%) of any income earned on contributions as school scholarships" — [IDOE SGO application](https://secure.in.gov/doe/files/sgo-application.pdf)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – Indiana]** School-side example: "the state allows Scholarships for Education Choice (the SGO) to use up to 10% of donations to fund administrative costs" — [Christian Academy of Indiana SGO FAQ 2025-26](https://caschools.us/wp-content/uploads/2025-2026-SGO-FAQ.pdf)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – Ohio OCEN]** "For this service a 9% administration fee is charged on each donation" — [Lake Center Christian School](https://www.lccs.com/support-lccs/sgo/); "All funds designated for a school, less the nine percent administrative fee, will go directly toward student scholarship at that school" — [Worthington Christian](https://www.worthingtonchristian.com/?p=104065); an older post cites 6.5% — [Worthington Christian (older)](https://www.worthingtonchristian.com/new-scholarship-granting-organization-helps-taxpayers-and-students-alike/); OCEN's parent CCV has launched a multistate "Christian Education Network" SGO for the federal credit — [CCV announcement](https://www.ccv.org/news/center-for-christian-virtue-expands-nationally-with-launch-of-christian-education-network-scholarship-organization)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – Georgia]** School marketing claims "Fellowship receives 100% of your redirected tax dollars" (school claim, not GOAL's fee schedule) — [Fellowship Christian School](https://www.fellowshipchristianschool.org/giving/ga-goal-clone); Georgia's state audit notes SSO "maximum administrative fee percentages are based on donations received" and a 94% obligation applies to SSOs with $10–20M in grants — [Georgia DOAA Special Examination 20-12](https://dlg.usg.edu/record/dlg_ggpd_s-ga-ba400-pp4-bm1-b2021-bs7-belec-p-btext); [Georgia SSO state laws (GaDOE)](https://www.gadoe.org:443/External-Affairs-and-Policy/Policy/Documents/SSO%20-%20STATE%20LAWS.pdf)

### Inferences
- The safest reading for 2027: a New York SGO may let a donor *express a preference* for Soundline so long as (i) the SGO retains discretion, (ii) no particular student is designated, (iii) the SGO as a whole still serves 10+ students at more than one school, and (iv) awards are made only to income-verified eligible students. Many state SGOs run exactly this "designated school fund" model. The school should not promise donors that a §25F gift "goes to Soundline" until Treasury answers the open question or the SGO's counsel signs off.
- A school-designated fund is operationally what makes a zero-student school fundable: donors tied to the school give in calendar 2027, the SGO holds the designated balance (less fee), and awards go to Soundline's enrolled, income-eligible families for 2027-28.

### Gaps
- The proposed-regulation preamble was not read in full; whether it requests comments on school designation is unverified.
- No New York SGO fee schedule exists yet for the federal credit.

## Key Question 4: Typical SGO fee structures, payout schedules, and how SGOs verify tuition and enrollment

### Takeaway
State caps cluster at 10% of contributions (Indiana 10%; Pennsylvania 10% since 1 July 2024; §25F itself requires ≥90% of *income* on scholarships), and actual SGO fees run 5–10% (OCEN 9%, Georgia average ~94% payout). Payment is typically made to the school, not the family, in installments (Step Up: quarterly, after enrollment verification and parent approval; GOAL: per executed three-party agreement, prorated refund on withdrawal), with the school supplying tuition/fee schedules and proof of admission.

### Cited Findings
- **[STATUTE]** SGO "cannot spend less than 90 percent of its income on scholarships for eligible students" — [IRS Notice 2025-70](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)
- **[PROP REG, via secondary]** "proposed § 1.25F-3(c)(4)(iii)(A) would require an organization to meet the 90 percent of income spending requirement as of the last day of the taxable year following the taxable year in which such income is received"; "Any award for a future year would only count toward satisfaction of the 90 percent of income spending requirement for the year in which it is paid" — [Federal Register, REG-117199-25 (via search extract)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/02/2026-20277/federal-scholarship-tax-credit); income "generally define[d] … as the organization's total gross receipts from all sources, unreduced by expenses," with a safe harbor "if at least 85 percent of the SGO's activities are scholarship-related" limiting the test to the segregated §25F account — [Dykema](https://www.dykema.com/news-insights/treasury-releases-section-25f-scholarship-tax-credit-regulations.html)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – Pennsylvania]** "new state requirements that went into effect beginning July 1, 2024, require that 90 percent or more of scholarship donations fund eligible scholarships, with 10 percent reserved for administration fees" (previously 80%); EITC scholarship organizations "spent an average of 93.8 percent of their funds on scholarships" — [Commonwealth Foundation, Sept 2024 paper](https://commonwealthfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Education-Tax-Credit-Scholarships-paper-Sept2024.pdf); [PA DCED EITC page](https://dced.pa.gov/programs/educational-improvement-tax-credit-program-eitc/)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – Indiana]** 10% administrative cap — [Ind. Code §20-51-3-3](https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/2015/title-20/article-51/chapter-3/)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – Ohio]** 9% fee on each donation — [Lake Center Christian School](https://www.lccs.com/support-lccs/sgo/)

## Key Question 5: Minimum feasible timeline (1 Dec 2026, 15 Jan 2027, 15 Feb 2027, before Sept 2027) for a Soundline family to receive a 2027 scholarship

### Takeaway
The binding dates are federal and state-level, not school-level: New York must (and has said it will) file its advance election by 1 Jan 2027 and its certified SGO list by 15 Feb 2027; donations count only once made on/after 1 Jan 2027 to a listed SGO; SGOs may spend 2027 income on scholarships through the end of 2028. For a September 2027 opening the school's own critical path is: be on an SGO's school roster by the time the SGO opens family applications (typically Jan–Mar in existing programs), have a BEDS code effective 1 July 2027, and have enrolled, income-verified families by summer 2027.

## Key Question 6: Alternative — a consortium of small Long Island independent schools/microschools forming its own SGO

### Takeaway
A consortium SGO is legally straightforward (10+ students across more than one school satisfies the "not all at the same school" test) but the timing is tight: a new 501(c)(3) needs Form 1023 approval (IRS currently taking roughly six months for full 1023s; 1023-EZ ~22 days but only for very small organizations), New York Charities Bureau registration before soliciting, and inclusion on New York's list by 15 Feb 2027 — although the temp regs allow a state to list a pending applicant if it certifies exemption will be effective by 1 January.

### Cited Findings
- **[STATUTE]** SGO must be "described in § 501(c)(3) and exempt from tax pursuant to § 501(a), and is not a private foundation"; must serve "10 or more students who do not all attend the same school" — [IRS Notice 2025-70](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)
- **[TEMP REG, via secondary]** "located in a State" = "authorized to do business in the State and … in compliance with the generally applicable State laws and requirements for charitable organizations in the State" — [Federal Register, T.D. 10057 (via extract)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/02/2026-20264/federal-scholarship-tax-credit); a state may list an organization with a pending exemption application if it certifies exempt status "will be effective by January 1" — [Thomson Reuters Tax](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/news/scholarship-groups-should-prepare-for-compliance-now-tax-pro-says/)
- **[PROP REG, via secondary]** 90% spending test satisfied "as of the last day of the taxable year following the taxable year in which such income is received" — a one-year cushion that "lets a startup satisfy the test by the end of its second year" — [Federal Register REG-117199-25 (via extract)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/02/2026-20277/federal-scholarship-tax-credit)
- **[IRS GUIDANCE]** IRS issues 80% of Form 1023-EZ determinations within 22 days and 80% of applications "requiring further review within 120 days"; it was "currently reviewing Form 1023-EZ applications submitted before February 24, 2026" — [IRS, Where's my application](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/wheres-my-application-for-tax-exempt-status); a secondary source reports 80% of full Form 1023s within 191 days and that as of a 28 June 2026 update the IRS had only assigned full 1023s submitted on or before 12 Jan 2026 — [Form1023.org processing time](https://form1023.org/how-long-the-irs-takes-to-process-form-1023-for-501c3); fees $600 (1023) / $275 (1023-EZ, limited to ≤$50,000 gross receipts and ≤$250,000 assets) — [Wylie Advisory](https://wylieadvisory.com/blog/1023-vs-1023-ez)
- **[NY]** New York Attorney General Charities Bureau: initial registration (CHAR410) "is required prior to any solicitation under Article 7-A"; $25 Article 7-A registration fee; religious organizations are exempt — [NY AG Charities registration](https://ag.ny.gov/resources/government-organizations/charities-nonprofits-fundraisers/charities-registration); [13 NYCRR 91-4 (Justia)](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-york/title-13/chapter-v/part-91/section-91-4); [Labyrinth guide](https://labyrinthinc.com/?p=30935)
- **[IRS GUIDANCE]** Colorado's SGO application (an analogue for what New York may ask) requires "a federal employer identification number (FEIN) and a 501(c)(3) determination letter, or IRS Form 1023 with proof of submission through Pay.gov if your application is pending" — [Colorado Dept. of Revenue SGOs](https://tax.colorado.gov/scholarship-granting-organizations)
- **[IRS GUIDANCE]** Once listed, SGOs "must register and report through the FSTC SGO Portal"; ongoing financial, recordkeeping, donor-acknowledgment and IRS reporting duties — [IRS FSTC page](https://www.irs.gov/government-entities/federal-state-local-governments/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-fstc); IRS is collecting comments on the SGO information-collection burden — [Federal Register, 21 Oct 2026 ICR notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/21/2026-21477/agency-information-collection-activities-requesting-comment-on-qualified-contributions-to-a)
- Practitioner advice: "independent schools may wish to consider the possibility of forming their own SGOs to support their own students … regional associations or groups of independent schools could possibly do the same, if permitted" — [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/08/federal-school-choice-program-2025-what-it-means); overview of whether to become or create an SGO — [Clark Nuber](https://clarknuber.com/articles/section-25f-scholarship-granting-organization-requirements/); SGO "rulebook" series — [Brown & Streza, Part 1](https://www.brownandstreza.com/the-new-sgo-rulebook-what-code-25f-and-the-treasury-regulations-require-of-scholarship-granting-organizations-part-1/)
- **[STATE PRACTICE]** Indiana bars an SGO from limiting scholarships to one school (IC 20-51-3-5) — [IDOE FAQ](https://secure.in.gov/tos/inesa/files/SGO-FAQ-for-Parents-and-Donors.pdf) — the same structural reason §25F requires students who "do not all attend the same school."

### Inferences
- A consortium SGO formed in November 2026 would likely not hold a 501(c)(3) determination letter by 15 Feb 2027 unless it qualifies for Form 1023-EZ (which its projected receipts may exceed). Inclusion on the 2027 list would therefore depend on New York choosing to exercise the "pending applicant" option and on the IRS back-dating exemption to formation (standard if filed within 27 months). This is feasible but risky; the lower-risk path for 2027 is to partner with an existing New York SGO and keep the consortium SGO as a 2028 project.
- An existing Long Island 501(c)(3) (e.g., an independent-school association, a community foundation, or a church-affiliated scholarship fund that is not a private foundation) could serve as the consortium's SGO immediately, avoiding the Form 1023 bottleneck, provided it opens a segregated §25F account and meets the 10-student/multi-school and 90% tests.

### Gaps
- No source documents the processing time for CHAR410 registration or whether New York will accept pending-exemption applicants on its first list.
- Whether any Long Island independent-school group is organizing an SGO could not be determined.

## Key Question 7: Family-side mechanics — applying to an SGO, income documentation at 300% of area median gross income, and coverage of the 2027-28 school year

### Takeaway
Families will apply to the SGO (not the IRS), and the SGO must verify prior-calendar-year household income and family size against 300% of HUD-style area median gross income for the Nassau-Suffolk area using tax returns/W-2s/pay stubs or categorical eligibility (SNAP, TANF, WIC, Section 8, SSI); the IRS plans to publish the applicable limits by area and family size. At 300% of Nassau-Suffolk AMI the ceiling will be very high (well over $300,000 for a family of four if the 4-person MFI is in the ~$160,000 range), so nearly all middle-income Miller Place families should qualify.

### Cited Findings
- **[STATUTE]** Eligible student: household income "for the calendar year prior to the date of the application for a scholarship, is not greater than 300 percent of the area median gross income (as such term is used in § 42)"; SGO must "verify the annual household income and family size" — [IRS Notice 2025-70](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)
- **[PROP REG, via secondary]** Proposed §1.25F-3(c)(6) would define AMGI consistently with §42 and require determining AMGI and household income "in a manner generally consistent with Section 8 determinations, including adjustments for family size"; the IRS "anticipates publishing the applicable limitations by geographic area and family size in guidance in the Internal Revenue Bulletin" — [Federal Register REG-117199-25 (via extract)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/02/2026-20277/federal-scholarship-tax-credit); [Tax990 summary](https://blog.tax990.com/2026/10/05/section-25f-scholarship-tax-credit-what-501c3-organizations-need-to-know-before-2027/)
- **[PROP REG, via secondary]** Verification may use "paystubs, tax returns, IRS transcripts, Forms W-2, or … crediting agencies or commercial data sources"; categorical eligibility via "SNAP, TANF, WIC, Section 8 housing, or SSI"; foster children need no separate verification; Treasury "also considering other safe harbors for students attending schools in low-income areas" — [Treasury Preview of Forthcoming Guidance](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/136/Preview-of-Forthcoming-Guidance.pdf); [AESA](https://www.aesa.us/2026/06/15/treasury-outlines-sgo-parameters-expected-in-regulations/); [Current Federal Tax Developments, June 2026](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2026/6/11/treasury-previews-regulatory-framework-for-new-section-25f-education-freedom-tax-credit)
- **[PROP REG, via secondary]** Student must be "eligible to enroll in a public elementary or secondary school, whether or not actually enrolled in one" — [EdChoice regulations summary](https://www.edchoice.org/2026-the-federal-scholarship-tax-credits-newly-released-regulations/)
- **[STATUTE]** Priority order: prior-year recipients first, then siblings of recipients — [IRS Notice 2025-70](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf)
- **Nassau-Suffolk income data (FY2026, effective 1 May 2026 / 1 June 2026 for HOME-CDBG):** NYS HCR's bedroom-adjusted table lists "Nassau-Suffolk, NY HUD Metro FMR Area" at $115,100 (0-bedroom column) rising to $190,600 (4-bedroom column), based on HUD income limits effective 05/01/2026 — [NYS HCR median income by bedroom count 2026](https://hcr.ny.gov/median-income-bedroom-count-2026); Town of Brookhaven's 2026 HUD limits for Suffolk/Nassau "are based on HUD estimates of Median Family Income (MFI), with adjustments based on family size," with the 80% row starting at $92,050 for one person — [Town of Brookhaven 2026 HUD Income Limits](https://www.brookhavenny.gov/367/View-Income-Guidelines); HUD released FY2026 limits on 1 May 2026 — [LeadingAge](https://leadingage.org/hud-posts-delayed-2026-income-limits-updates-fair-market-rents/); [PHADA](https://www.phada.org/Publications/Breaking-News-Administrator/View/ArticleId/18594/HUD-Releases-2026-Income-Limits)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – Illinois]** Families applied through an online reservation then portal; awards depended "on available funds at their school of choice"; proof of residency required — [Empower Illinois reminders](https://www.jca-online.org/?p=12003); [Archdiocese of Chicago TCS application checklist](https://schools.archchicago.org/documents/80540/4262901/TCS+Application+Checklist_EN/d9f24d50-c7d6-402e-859d-4b7987be05a6)
- **[STATE PRACTICE – NY]** Existing New York diocesan aid uses FACTS with document verification and a $45 fee — [Futures in Education flyer](https://files.tilmaplatform.com/system/resources/W1siZiIsIjIwMjYvMDEvMjYvOGtnY3c3eTV3el8yMDI2XzIwMjdfRnV0dXJlc19pbl9FZHVjYXRpb25fRmluYW5jaWFsX0FpZF9GbHllcl9FbmcucGRmIl1d/2026-2027%20Futures%20in%20Education%20Financial%20Aid%20Flyer%20Eng.pdf)

### Inferences
- The HCR "bedroom count" table follows the LIHTC convention (0BR = 1 person … 4BR = 6 persons), so the $115,100 and $190,600 figures are *not* the 4-person MFI; the Brookhaven 80%-limit row ($92,050 for 1 person) implies a 4-person 80% limit around $131,000 and therefore a 4-person Nassau-Suffolk MFI roughly in the $160,000–$165,000 range (the researcher's arithmetic, not a published figure). At 300% that is a ceiling on the order of $480,000+ for a family of four; the IRS tables will govern. Report writer should present the ceiling as "well above $300,000" and flag that the official 2027 table has not been published.
- For a family applying in spring 2027 for 2027-28, the SGO will verify calendar-2026 income (the "calendar year prior to the date of the application"), typically from the 2026 Form 1040/W-2s, which will exist by Feb–Apr 2027.
- The sibling/prior-recipient priority is irrelevant in year one but will matter for Soundline families in 2028-29 and beyond.

### Gaps
- HUD's own FY2026 Nassau-Suffolk 4-person MFI figure was not retrieved (huduser.gov not fetched); the IRS has not yet published the §25F limitation tables.
- Whether an SGO may accept a FACTS/SSS financial-aid file in lieu of its own verification is not addressed in the sources.
