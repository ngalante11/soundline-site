Where the name comes from

Before sonar, sailors dropped a sounding line over the side of the boat. It was a rope with a lead weight on the end, and it told them how deep the water was before they picked a direction. We do the same with your child. First we find out where they really are in each subject, and what they love. Then we pick a direction together.

A day here

What a school day looks like.

School runs 8:30 to 3:00, Monday to Friday.

8:30 to 10:30

Math, reading and writing.

We call it practice. Each child works at their own level, with a guide at the table. It is hands-on. The only screen in the room is the guide’s.

10:30 to 3:00

Classes, labs and projects.

We call it making. History, literature and science are taught by a person, with books, not by an app. There is lunch, time outside, and the arts every day.

Every six weeks

Then they show it.

Each project lasts six weeks and is made for a real person in town. At the end your child stands up and explains it. No slides.

A week at Soundline

The schedule is the same every week, so the work can change.

Every morning at 10:30 the whole school sits down together with one real problem from town.

Extended Day, 3:00 to 5:30. A lesson at 3:15, then a homework desk until 5:30. It is for five-day students. It costs $330 a month over twelve months, or $297 for Founding Families, and you pay only if you use it.

A Tuesday

One seventh grader’s Tuesday, hour by hour.

This is a Tuesday in September, the way we plan to run it. We follow one student from the door to pickup. There is no name, because our first students start in September 2027.

8:30Arrival
The door.

The same adult greets them every morning. Bag on the hook, phone in the box, a word about the weekend.

8:45Morning meeting
Fifteen minutes with their guide.

A guide is our word for a teacher. Ten to twelve students meet with the guide who stays with them for three years. Each sets a goal for the day. Today’s: pass the check on proportions, and draft a question for science lab.

9:00Practice · 50 min
Math, at their own level.

A student moves to the next skill when they can show a guide they have it, not when the calendar says so. Across the table, a sixth grader is working on a skill this student passed in week one. Beside them, an eighth grader is on one they will reach by winter.

9:50Practice · 40 min
Reading and writing.

Today they read about how ice carved Long Island Sound, because science lab needs it. Then they write two paragraphs. One has to make an argument.

10:30Whole school · 30 min
Everyone together. Today, math.

Every morning at 10:30 the whole school sits down with one real problem from town and talks it through. We call it seminar. Tuesday is math. One student proves why a shortcut works. This student finds the case where it doesn’t, on the fourth try.

11:00Outside · 40 min
Lunch and outside.

Kids go outside every day, whatever the weather. A guide learns a lot about a student out here.

11:40Science lab · 95 min
Building a depth sensor.

They are building a sensor on a line that measures how deep a tank of water is. Today they test it, and the graph bends where it shouldn’t. On Friday they explain why, out loud.

1:15Project time · 60 min
The six-week project.

We call it a Quest. This is week three of six. The children’s librarian asked for an exhibit on how sound travels through water. The group has a plan, forty dollars, and a speaker that won’t stay sealed. A guide is in the room and does not fix it for them.

2:15Music · 45 min
The school band.

Everyone plays something. Nobody has to be good yet. This student has the bass part.

3:00Extended Day
Home, or Extended Day.

Pickup is at 3:00. Or your child stays: a lesson at 3:15, then a homework desk until 5:30. The project stays at school.

Why it works

Each part has been studied for decades. Putting them in one school is new.

A child moves on when they have it.

More than a hundred studies show this works better than moving the whole class on a schedule. Students who struggle gain the most.

Long projects raise test scores.

In controlled studies, students who learned through long projects scored higher on state tests than students taught the usual way.

Writing and talking make it stick.

Students remember what they have to write about and explain out loud.

Kids work harder for an adult who knows them.

One close relationship with a teacher is among the strongest predictors of how hard a student tries, and it matters more as kids get older. That is why one adult stays with your child for three years.

Questions parents ask

The ones that come up first.

Is this a real school or a homeschool program?

It is a school. Your child is enrolled here five days a week, we teach every subject New York requires, and you file no homeschool paperwork. We also run a separate three-day program for families who homeschool.

How many students will there be?

Ten to twelve in a grade. Nobody is stuck in their grade, because students work at their own level across the school. On Fridays the whole school builds one thing together. It is few enough that no child gets overlooked, and enough for a real class discussion.

What does it cost?

Five days a week is $1,500 a month, $18,000 a year. Founding Families, the first families to join, pay $1,350 a month. Three days a week is $900 a month, and Founding Families pay $810. It is paid monthly over twelve months, and Founding Families keep their price through twelfth grade. Every price is two pages on.

Do students take the state tests?

Yes. The state tests are in the spring and take three mornings. We do not do test prep. New York uses the tests to check that a new school is teaching what it should. All year long you will also see how much your own child has grown, three times a year.

What about high school?

Ninth grade opens here in September 2028. Then we add a grade every year through twelfth. Your child can stay, or move on with a folder of real work that shows what they can do.

Is Soundline approved by New York?

New York does not license private schools. A private school registers with the state, and the local district reviews what it teaches. We do both. We are not accredited yet. We start that process once we are open.

How much screen time is there?

Very little. Morning math, reading and writing are hands-on, at a table with a guide. The only screen in the morning belongs to the guide. Class discussions, science lab and presentations happen without screens.

Where will the school be?

On the North Shore, along the Miller Place–Port Jefferson Station corridor. Port Jefferson Station is the likeliest. We announce the address the day we sign the lease.

What if the school doesn’t open?

You get your money back. Your $500 is kept by an independent escrow agent, not by us, and it comes back in full, for any reason, until the day we sign a lease. If the school doesn’t open after that, we mail you a check.

We homeschool. Can we join?

Yes. The Collaborative is three days a week: Monday and Wednesday, 9:15 to 12:30, and Friday, 9:15 to 2:45, with the same guides and the same rooms. You keep your homeschool plan, and we hand you course descriptions and hours logs to go with it. Every child who takes classes here, three days or five, has to meet New York’s school immunization requirements.

Will our district bus my child?

Most likely. New York districts bus students to private schools up to fifteen miles away. You ask your own district by April 1, and we send you the request already filled in.

My child has an IEP. What happens to services?

Your child can still receive services from the public school district. You request them from the district where the school is located by June 1, and we will help you do it.

Can my child still play school sports?

New York requires students to be enrolled in the district to play on district teams, so town and club teams are the way to go. That is where most students this age already play. We have PE in the school day, and Extended Day runs until 5:30.

My child is younger, or older. Can we still talk?

Yes. A new sixth grade starts every September, and sign-up for September 2028 opens in August 2027. Write to us and we will call you before sign-up opens for your child’s year. We will not have a grade for students who are in eighth grade or higher this school year.

Something we didn’t answer? Write to hello@soundlineschool.com. If we don’t know, we will find out and tell you.
