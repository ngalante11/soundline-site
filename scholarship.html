The federal scholarship tax credit, starting January 1, 2027

A federal scholarship starts the January before we open.

For the first time, there is federal money for private school tuition — and it arrives in the same school year your child would start here.

What we know so far.

You should not have to guess at this.

Last year’s federal tax law created a scholarship tax credit that begins January 1, 2027. It works through non-profit scholarship granting organizations: a taxpayer gives to one and takes a dollar-for-dollar credit on their federal return, up to $1,700 a person. The organization awards the money to families as tuition scholarships.

Two things make this matter on the North Shore. The first is the income line: a household qualifies at or below 300% of area median gross income. On Long Island the area median for a family of four runs around $164,000, which puts the ceiling near half a million dollars. This is not a low-income program here. It is most families.

The second is the calendar. Each state has to opt in, and New York’s governor said on October 9 that New York will. The state’s election has to be filed with the IRS by January 1, and the list of approved organizations is due February 15 — weeks after you would be choosing a school for September.

We are not going to overpromise a program that is not final. New York has announced and not yet filed. What we will do is tell you the day it is official, name the scholarship organization we work with the day the state’s list is published, and sit down with you and the actual numbers before you sign anything.

Your federal taxes can pay for a neighbor’s seat.

Not a donation in the usual sense. A dollar-for-dollar federal credit of up to $1,700 a taxpayer means the money leaves your taxes instead of your pocket, and lands on a local family’s tuition. Twenty neighbors is a scholarship. A hundred is a scholarship fund.

  • It is a credit, not a deduction.

    Up to $1,700 a person, dollar for dollar, non-refundable, starting with the 2027 tax year.
