# The asks

Written 12 Nov 2026. One script per kind of institution. Companion to the Sources tab, where every ask gets a row before it is made.

**The frame, every time.** Give first, ask for one thing, put a date on it. The gift is a free ninety-minute build for their kids, Adam running it, or the scholarship session for their parents. The one thing is specific: a date on their calendar, a line in their newsletter, a flyer on their counter. "Please share" is not an ask and "a talk about our school" is not a gift.

**Thirty seconds, in person**, which is the version that works: *"I'm opening a small middle school in Miller Place next September. Before I ask anyone for anything, I'd like to give your [kids / families] something: a free Saturday build, instruments out of junk, run by a guy who does these for libraries all over the Island. Could we put one on your calendar for [date]?"*

Log the answer in Sources the same day: Yes, No, or Waiting, and the date. A yes gets its own flyer tag (`python3 tools/qr.py <tag>`, then `node tools/flyers.js <tag>`), so that wall's QR code says where the family came from.

## Library (Adam)

**Email:**
> Subject: A free Saturday program for your 10-to-13s, November through January
>
> [Name], I'd like to offer the library a Saturday series this winter at no cost: ninety-minute builds for ages ten to thirteen, the same instrument lab and escape-room formats I've run at [libraries he's run them at], ending each time with the children presenting to their parents. I'd need the meeting room from 9:30 to 12:00 on [dates], and a line in the calendar and the newsletter. The series is run by Soundline, the new middle school opening on Long Island’s North Shore next September, which I'm part of; the sessions themselves are programs, not presentations. Could we take [first date]?

The library is the one place where "free program" is the gift and the ask in one sentence. Ask for the newsletter line explicitly; the calendar listing alone reaches people already in the building.

## Scout leader (Nicole; Gina for Girl Scouts)

**Email:**
> Subject: A build night for [troop/pack/den], free, that counts toward something
>
> [Name], I run a new middle school opening on Long Island’s North Shore next September, and one of our guides, Adam King, runs instrument labs and escape rooms for libraries across Long Island. I'd like to give the [troop / Arrow of Light den / Cadettes] a ninety-minute build night at no cost, on a date you pick, and you decide what it counts toward; we'll shape it to fit. Children build something that works and present it. No pitch; the school comes up if a parent asks. Would [date] or [date] work for a meeting?

Arrow of Light scouts are sixth graders in September 2027. Cadettes are grades six through eight exactly. The leader decides what the build counts toward; never claim it yourself.

## Youth pastor (Nicole)

**Email:**
> Subject: A free build night for your youth group, from a neighbor
>
> [Name], I'm a neighbor opening a small, non-sectarian middle school in Miller Place next September. I'm not writing to ask for your pulpit or your families. I'd like to give the youth group a ninety-minute evening, instruments out of scrap or an escape-room build, run by Adam King, who does these for libraries across the Island. The kids make something and present it. If you'd rather it was simply a good night for them with no school mentioned at all, that is fine with me; I'd rather earn the second conversation than force the first. Would an evening in [month] work?

Run one of the three churches before the other two, and read the room. Non-sectarian cuts both ways with these families; the build is the only argument worth making.

## Bookstore owner (Nicole, with Izzy)

**In person, then email:**
> We'd like to bring you Saturday traffic. Izzy, who designs how our school teaches and co-wrote New York's framework for virtual schools, would run a forty-minute live seminar for ten-to-thirteen-year-olds on a book you sell, in your space, on a Saturday [morning / afternoon]. Parents browse; we end with ten minutes for them. You pick the book from your stock. We bring the children and the flyers. Would [date] work?

This replaces the information night. The seminar is the demonstration; the debrief is the information.

## Orthodontist or pediatrician front desk (Gina)

**In person, with both flyers in hand:**
> I'm from Soundline, the new middle school opening on Long Island’s North Shore next September. We're running free Saturday builds for ten-to-thirteen-year-olds this winter. Could these two go on your board? One's for the kids in the waiting room and one's for the parents. I'll come by and swap them when the dates change.

Nearly every child in the grade band sits in one of these rooms this winter with a parent and twenty minutes. Each practice gets its own tag.

## Studio or club owner (Gina)

**In person:**
> You know these families better than anyone and you've known them for years. I'm not asking you to recommend us; I'm asking for the counter and one date. The kids' flyer by the sign-in sheet, and if you'd let us, a Saturday build for your ten-to-thirteens in your space in January, free, run by Adam. They'll talk about it in class for a week.

For Gina's own families, the ask is a personal note, not a flyer: one paragraph, the next build date, her name at the bottom.

## Shop window, Main Street (Gina)

**In person:** "Would you put this in the window for the Dickens weekend? It's a free kids' build at the library two blocks from here, that Saturday morning." The kid flyer only. Asked for, never taped.

## PTA bulletin (Nicole)

**Email to the PTA president, not the school:**
> Subject: One line for the November bulletin: a free Saturday build for middle schoolers
>
> [Name], would the bulletin take one line? "Free Saturday builds for ages 10 to 13, instruments out of junk, 10:00 to 11:30 from November 21, soundlineschool.com/build." It's run by Soundline, a new independent middle school opening on Long Island’s North Shore next September. Happy to send a flyer for the table too.

A PTA will carry a free community event for children. It will not carry a school advertisement. Write the line so that it is the first thing.

## Homeschool co-op or group admin (Nicole)

**Post or email:**
> For the families here with ten-to-thirteen-year-olds: three free mornings over winter break, December 29 to 31, 9:15 to 12:30, one build across the three days and a presentation to parents on the 31st. Run by Adam King (library instrument labs and escape rooms across the Island) and Izzy Galante. It's from Soundline, the new school opening on Long Island’s North Shore next September, whose three-day Collaborative runs on exactly these hours; the Quest is a way to try the shape of it with no commitment. Spots: soundlineschool.com/build.

Lead with the hours. For a homeschool family the schedule is the pitch, and "by the term, not the year" is the close when they ask.

## What never to say

- "A talk about our school." It's a build, a seminar or a session on the scholarship. The school is the second half.
- "Please share this." Ask for the counter, the line, the date.
- "We're still working that out" without a date. "The address comes with your confirmation" is an answer. "Soon" is a leak.
- Anything about the building being likely. It is signed or it is not.
