Questions families ask.

Money
What is a founding rate, exactly?

The tuition you enroll at is the tuition you pay, every year your child is here, through twelfth grade. It does not rise with our costs, and it does not rise when the school gets harder to get into. Founding families carry their rate the whole way.

How is it paid?

Monthly, over twelve months, starting the summer before your child walks in. Not two lump sums in August and January.

What if the school doesn’t open?

Your $500 comes back in full, for any reason, until the day we sign a lease. If the school doesn’t open after that, your money comes back too: we mail you a check.

Is there financial aid of your own?

Yes, and it is a real line in our first budget rather than an afterthought. Tell us that cost is the question for you. It changes how we build the aid pool, and it does not count against your child.

Is busing really free?

Your own district provides it to a private school up to fifteen miles from your home, at their cost, if you request it by April 1. We will send you the request already filled in.

The school
How many students will there be?

Ten to twelve in a grade — and nobody’s stuck in their grade. Students work by skill across the school, and on Fridays the whole school builds one thing together. Few enough that there’s nowhere to hide, enough for a real seminar.

Where will the school be?

On the North Shore, along the Miller Place–Port Jefferson Station corridor. Port Jefferson Station is the likeliest. We announce the address the day we sign the lease.

What about high school?

Ninth grade opens here in September 2028, then a grade every year through twelfth. New York has moved to a diploma built on evidence of six things a graduate can do, and every one of them is something your child makes, defends, or shows here, every six weeks.

How much screen time is there?

Less than you’d think. Morning practice is hands-on, at a table with a guide; the only screen in the morning belongs to the guide. Seminar, lab, and the defense happen without screens. AI is taught as a subject, never used as a shortcut.

New York
Is Soundline approved by New York?

New York doesn’t license private schools. You register with the state, and the district reviews that what you teach is equivalent. We do all of it, then start accreditation once we’re open.

Do students take the state tests?

Yes — the state tests in the spring, three mornings, no test prep. New York uses them to confirm a new school is teaching what it should, and we’d rather show that than argue about it. What you’ll see all year is growth, three times a year, for your own child.

How do you screen the adults who work with my child?

Every adult who works with students is fingerprinted through the New York State Education Department before they start, and is checked against the sex offender registry. Everyone completes mandated reporter training. There are always at least two adults in the building.

My child has an IEP. What happens to services?

Your child can still receive services from the public school district. You request them from the district where the school is located by June 1, and we’ll help you do it.

A federal scholarship starts the January before we open.

A new federal tax credit for private school scholarships begins January 1, 2027. New York has said it will opt in and has not yet filed. We have written up everything we know.
