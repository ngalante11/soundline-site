The Collaborative · A three-day homeschool program for grades 6–8 on Long Island’s North Shore

You keep teaching your child. We take Monday, Wednesday, Friday.

The Collaborative is Soundline School three days a week, for homeschooling families on the North Shore. Your child has the same guides (our word for teachers), the same rooms and the same Friday project as the full-time students. The rest of the week, your child is home with you.

You keep your own homeschool plan and file your own reports. We hand you course descriptions and hours logs to go with them.

The two things a kitchen table cannot do.

A good discussion needs eight kids who read the same book and disagree. A science lab needs equipment nobody buys for one child.

Most families who homeschool a twelve-year-old are not short on instruction. They are short on two specific things: a room of peers who will argue with your child about a book, and a workshop with tools in it.

That is what the three days are for. Monday and Wednesday, 9:15 to 12:30, are math, writing and the long project, at a table with a guide who knows exactly where your child is. Friday, 9:15 to 2:45, is the whole school together, full-time and three-day students, building one thing and showing it at 2:00. Nobody is a visitor on Friday.

Your child works at their own level, whatever their age and whether they come three days or five. A three-day seventh grader sits at the same table as a full-time eighth grader working on the same skill.

The paperwork is the part we can actually take off you.

New York asks a homeschooling family for an IHIP, four quarterly reports, and an annual assessment. Three of those four get easier when a third of the week is documented by someone else. We write it in the form your district already reads.

  • Course descriptions that match the IHIP.

    For every subject your child touches here, written in syllabus form, before you file.

  • Hours and attendance logs, quarterly.

    Dated, by subject, ready to paste into your quarterly report.

  • Real work, not just grades.

    Every six weeks your child presents a piece of work and answers questions about it. You get the scoring sheet, the result and the work itself. That is exactly what an annual assessment asks for.

Questions homeschooling families ask.

Do we stop being a homeschooling family?

No. You remain the educator of record. Your IHIP is yours, your district relationship is yours, and your child’s program is yours to set. We are three days of it, documented.

Does this fit New York’s homeschool rules?

Yes, and the schedule is built around the rule. New York lets a homeschooling family use group instruction for part of the program, as long as it is not the majority of it. The state asks for 900 hours a year in sixth grade and 990 in seventh and eighth. The Collaborative is about 432, so most of your child’s instruction stays with you, and so does your IHIP. If you would like your district to confirm that before you commit, we will give you the hours in writing to show them.

Does my child need to be vaccinated?

Yes. Soundline is a school, and New York’s school immunization law covers every child who attends classes in a school building, whether they come three days or five. That includes homeschooled students taking classes here. The only exemption New York allows is a medical one. We would rather you hear that from us now than find out in August.

Which days, and what hours?

Monday and Wednesday, 9:15 to 12:30. Friday, 9:15 to 2:45. Friday runs longer on purpose: the whole school builds one thing together that morning and shows it at 2:00, and your child presents with everyone else. It is the day we would ask you not to skip. Altogether that is about 432 hours a year, under half of what New York asks of a homeschooling family in any of our grades, so you stay the educator of record.

How many Collaborative seats are there?

A few alongside each grade, so every child stays known. Tell us you are interested and we will tell you exactly where you stand, by name, rather than leaving you to wonder.

Which grades?

Grades six through eight for our first year, then a grade a year through twelfth, the same as the full-time school.

What does it cost?

Three days a week is $900 a month, $10,800 a year, paid over twelve months. Founding families pay $810 a month.

Can we try a term?

Yes. The Collaborative is enrolled by the term, not the year. Full-time enrollment is annual; three days is not, because we know you are testing whether it fits.

Can we move to full-time later?

Yes, if there is a seat, and Collaborative families get first refusal on one. Going the other way is fine too. Some years need more kitchen table, not less.

Will our district bus my child?

Transportation rules are written for enrolled private school students, so a Collaborative child is a different case and it varies by district. Tell us your district and we will find out the real answer for yours before you have to ask them.

Does my child have to be ahead, or behind, or anything?

No. We find out where your child is in each subject and move them on when they have it. That is how the whole school works, and it is why kids from different backgrounds learn well in the same room.
