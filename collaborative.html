The two things a kitchen table cannot do.

A seminar needs eight people who read the same thing and disagree. A lab needs equipment nobody buys for one child.

Most families who homeschool a twelve-year-old are not short on instruction. They are short on two specific things: a room of peers who will argue with your child about a book, and a workshop with tools in it.

That is what the three days are. Monday and Wednesday are practice and studio — math, writing, and the long project, at a table with a guide who knows where your child actually is. Friday is the whole school, full-time and Collaborative together, building one thing. Nobody is a visitor on Friday.

Your child is grouped by skill, not age, and not by whether they come three days or five. A Collaborative seventh grader sits at the same table as a full-time eighth grader working the same skill.

The paperwork is the part we can actually take off you.

New York asks a homeschooling family for an IHIP, four quarterly reports, and an annual assessment. Three of those four get easier when a third of the week is documented by someone else.

We write it in the form your district already reads.

  • Course descriptions that match the IHIP. For every subject your child touches here, written in syllabus form, before you file.
  • Hours and attendance logs, quarterly. Dated, by subject, ready to paste into your quarterly report.
  • Evidence, not grades. Every six weeks your child defends a piece of work. You get the rubric, the result, and the artifact — which is exactly what an annual assessment wants to see.

Questions homeschooling families ask.

Do we stop being a homeschooling family?

No. You remain the educator of record. Your IHIP is yours, your district relationship is yours, and your child's program is yours to set. We are three days of it, documented.

Which days, and what hours?

Monday, Wednesday, and Friday, 8:30 to 3:00, with Extended Day to 5:30 if you want it on those days. Friday is the whole-school build, which is the day we would ask you not to skip.

How many Collaborative seats are there?

A small number alongside each grade — the point is that the room stays small. Say you are interested on the survey and we will tell you exactly where you stand, by name, rather than leaving you to wonder.

Which grades?

Grades six through eight for our first year, then a grade a year through twelfth, the same as the full-time school.

What does it cost?

Less than full enrollment, priced as three days rather than five, paid monthly over twelve months. The number comes on November 12 with everything else. See what it costs, including the federal scholarship that starts in January 2027.

Can we try a term?

Yes. The Collaborative is enrolled by the term, not the year. Full-time enrollment is annual; three days is not, because we know you are testing whether it fits.

Can we move to full-time later?

Yes, if there is a seat, and Collaborative families get first refusal on one. Going the other way is fine too — some years need more kitchen table, not less.

Will our district bus my child?

Transportation rules are written for enrolled private school students, so a Collaborative child is a different case and it varies by district. Tell us your district on the survey and we will find out the real answer for yours before you have to ask them.

Does my child have to be ahead, or behind, or anything?

No. Grouped by skill means we meet your child where they are in each subject and move them when they have it. That is the whole design, and it is the reason mixed-background rooms work here.

If you homeschool and you are curious, write to hello@soundlineschool.com and say so. A real person who can answer homeschool questions will write back.
