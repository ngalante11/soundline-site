A day at Soundline School · 8:30 to 3:00

Mornings are for skills. The rest of the day is for real work.

Every morning there are two hours of math, reading and writing, each child at their own level. We call it practice. The rest of the day is classes, labs and projects. We call it making. School ends at 3:00, and your child can stay until 5:30 if you need it.

One seventh grader’s Tuesday, hour by hour.

This is a Tuesday in September, the way we plan to run it. We follow one student from the door to pickup. There is no name, because our first students start in September 2027.

PracticeMakingExtended Day
  1. 8:30
    Arrival

    The door.

    The same adult greets them every morning. Bag on the hook, phone in the box, a word about the weekend.

  2. 8:45
    Morning meeting

    Fifteen minutes with their guide.

    A guide is our word for a teacher. Ten to twelve students meet with the guide who stays with them for three years. Each sets a goal for the day. Today’s: pass the check on proportions, and draft a question for science lab.

  3. 9:00
    Practice, 50 min

    Math, at their own level.

    A student moves to the next skill when they can show a guide they have it, not when the calendar says so. Across the table, a sixth grader is working on a skill this student passed in week one. Beside them, an eighth grader is on one they will reach by winter.

  4. 9:50
    Practice, 40 min

    Reading and writing.

    Today they read about how ice carved Long Island Sound, because science lab needs it. Then they write two paragraphs. One has to make an argument.

  5. 10:30
    Whole school, 30 min

    Everyone together. Today, math.

    Every morning at 10:30 the whole school sits down with one real problem from town and talks it through. We call it seminar. Tuesday is math. One student proves why a shortcut works. This student finds the case where it doesn’t, on the fourth try.

  6. 11:00
    Outside, 40 min

    Lunch and outside.

    Kids go outside every day, whatever the weather. A guide learns a lot about a student out here.

  7. 11:40
    Science lab, 95 min

    Building a depth sensor.

    They are building a sensor on a line that measures how deep a tank of water is. Today they test it, and the graph bends where it shouldn’t. On Friday they explain why, out loud.

  8. 1:15
    Project time, 60 min

    The six-week project.

    We call it a Quest. This is week three of six. The children’s librarian asked for an exhibit on how sound travels through water. The group has a plan, forty dollars, and a speaker that won’t stay sealed. A guide is in the room and does not fix it for them.

  9. 2:15
    Music, 45 min

    The school band.

    Everyone plays something. Nobody has to be good yet. This student has the bass part.

  10. 3:00
    To 5:30, if you need it

    Home, or Extended Day.

    Pickup is at 3:00. Or your child stays: a lesson at 3:15, then a homework desk until 5:30. The project stays at school.

How the day adds up.

  • Practice: two hours.

    Ninety minutes of math, reading and writing at their own level, then thirty minutes talking a problem through together. It is hands-on, at a table with a guide. The only screen in the morning belongs to the guide.

  • Making: four hours.

    Science lab, the project, music, and lunch with forty minutes outside. It is taught by a person, and the project is built for someone who asked for it.

  • Where it was hard.

    The fourth try at 10:30, and the graph that has to be explained on Friday. The guide knows which one was hard for this student.

  • What they keep.

    The math skill, if they passed it. The paragraph. The graph. Every student keeps a folder of real work. By eighth grade it shows what they can do. This Tuesday is one page of it.

A week at Soundline.

The schedule is the same every week, so the work can change. Every morning at 10:30 the whole school sits down together with one real problem from town.

Monday

The plan

At 8:45 each student sets one goal and one plan for the week.

Tuesday

Science lab, then projects

Ninety minutes of hands-on science, written up before they leave the lab. Then everyone works on their project: a video game, a robot, a portrait, a sculpture, a song.

Wednesday

Books

The long discussion of the week: a novel, a question, and a paragraph. Every discussion ends in writing.

Thursday

Apprenticeships

Seventh and eighth graders work alongside an adult who does a job for a living. Sixth graders work on their project.

Friday

One big build, then the show

The whole school builds one thing together: a song, a mural, a project for someone in town. At 2:00 students present their work and something is performed. It is usually loud. Families are welcome.
