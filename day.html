A day at Soundline School · 8:30 to 3:00

Two hours of practice. Four hours of making.

Mornings are for skills, at each student’s own pace. Afternoons are for real work with a real audience. Then everyone goes home, or stays for Extended Day.

Practice 8:30, two hours
Making Four hours, to 3:00
Extended Day To 5:30, if you need it

One seventh grader. One day. Hour by hour.

September, the first six-week cycle. The Tuesday we’ll run, followed through one student’s day. No name, because it isn’t any one child’s day yet.

PracticeMakingExtended Day
  1. 8:30
    Arrival

    The door.

    The same face every morning. Bag on the hook, phone in the box, a word about the weekend.

  2. 8:45
    Launch

    Advisory, fifteen minutes.

    Ten to twelve students, one guide for three years. Today’s goal: clear the proportions gate, draft the lab question.

  3. 9:00
    Practice, 50 min

    Math, at their own gate.

    The gate opens when they can show it to a guide, not when the unit ends. Across the table, a sixth grader is on a gate this student cleared in week one; beside them, an eighth grader is on one they’ll reach by winter.

  4. 9:50
    Practice, 40 min

    Reading and writing.

    Today’s text: how ice carved the Sound, because the lab needs it. Two paragraphs, one an argument.

  5. 10:30
    Seminar, 30 min

    The seminar. Today, math.

    Every morning at 10:30 the whole school sits down with one real thing from town and one question, and the students name the skills it takes. Tuesday is math: someone proves why the shortcut works; this student finds the case where it doesn’t. The fourth try lands.

  6. 11:00
    Outside, 40 min

    Lunch and outside.

    Outside, every day, whatever the weather. Most of what a guide learns about a student, they learn here.

  7. 11:40
    Lab, 95 min

    The sounder.

    A sensor on a line that reads the depth of a tank: the sounding line, rebuilt. Today is calibration, and the graph bends where it shouldn’t. Defended Friday.

  8. 1:15
    Studio, 60 min

    The Quest.

    Week three of six. The client is the children’s librarian: an exhibit on how sound travels through water. The group has a plan, forty dollars, and a speaker that won’t stay sealed. A guide is in the room and does not fix it.

  9. 2:15
    Ensemble, 45 min

    The ensemble.

    Everyone plays something; nobody has to be good yet. The bass part is this student’s.

  10. 3:00
    To 5:30, if you need it

    Home, or not yet.

    A lesson at 3:15, then the homework desk until 5:30. Or the carline. The quest stays at school.

Imagined. Our first students arrive in September 2027.

How the day adds up.

  • Practice: two hours.

    Ninety minutes at their own gate, thirty out loud. Hands-on, at a table with a guide; the only screen in the morning belongs to the guide.

  • Making: four hours.

    Lab, quest, ensemble, and forty minutes outside. Taught by a person, built for someone who asked.

  • Where the stretch is.

    The fourth try at 10:30, and Friday’s graph. The guide knows which one is hard.

  • What goes in the folder.

    The gate, if it opened. The paragraph. The graph. By eighth grade the folder is a portfolio; this Tuesday is one page.

A week at Soundline.

Same rhythm every week, so the work can be different. Every morning at 10:30, the whole school: one real thing from town, one question, and the students name the skills it takes.

Monday

Launch

8:45. One question, one goal, one plan for the week.

Tuesday

Lab, then studio

Ninety minutes, hands on, written up before studio. Then everyone makes something: a video game, a robot, a portrait, a sculpture, a song for the ensemble.

Wednesday

Seminar

The long one. A novel, a question, a paragraph. Every seminar ends in writing.

Thursday

Apprenticeships

Grades 7 and 8 work alongside someone who does it for a living. Younger students build their quest.

Friday

All In, then Showcase

The whole school takes on one thing together. A song, a mural, a project for someone in town. At 2:00, someone presents and something is performed. Usually loud. Families welcome.
