About Soundline School, a new private middle school

Every adult here knows your kid.

There are ten to twelve students in a grade. One adult stays with your child for three years. And the four people starting the school will know your child by name.

Where the name comes from.

Before sonar, sailors dropped a sounding line over the side of the boat. It was a rope with a lead weight on the end, and it told them how deep the water was. A dab of grease on the weight brought up a bit of the bottom (sand, shell, mud), so they knew what was down there too. Only then did they pick a direction.

That is how we get to know your child. We find out where they really are in each subject. We also find out what they love, and how far it goes. Middle schoolers do well when an adult truly knows what they need.

Then we pick a direction together. What your child loves becomes part of the work: the next skill, the next project, the next thing worth showing their classmates and their town. That gives a student more than a grade on a report card.

We teach everything New York requires. What we learn about your child is what we build on.

Depth with direction.

Ten to twelve in a grade, and nobody is stuck in it.

Students work at their own level across the school, and on Fridays the whole school builds one thing together. It is few enough that no child gets overlooked, and enough for a real class discussion.

  • One adult for three years.

    Every student has the same guide (our word for a teacher) looking out for them from the day they start, in a group of ten to twelve.

  • Each child works at their own level.

    A sixth grader and an eighth grader can work on the same skill at the same table. Each moves on when they have it.

  • Three grades, one building.

    We add a grade every year through twelfth, starting with ninth in September 2028. Older students spend part of the week out in town, working alongside adults.

The people starting it.

Founder

An educator for over twenty years. She started as a classroom teacher, coached teachers in New York City schools for more than fifteen of them, and led the state team that wrote New York’s Quality Remote and Hybrid Teaching Framework and its Handbook for Virtual Schools.

Lead guide

Adam King

A musician and teacher. He designs and runs music and STEAM programs for libraries across Long Island (escape rooms, instrument labs, world percussion) and teaches private lessons. He’ll know your child by Wednesday.

Operations

For more than thirty-five years she has taught dance to North Shore kids, many of them right at this age, and she has run businesses here her whole life, including her own salon. She makes sure every family feels seen, and handles every detail in between.
