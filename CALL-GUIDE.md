# The sounding call

Twenty minutes on the phone with one family. 28 Oct 2026.

---

## Why this is the funnel right now

Zero came to the 25 October talk. One phone call turned into a second conversation. Until there is a list, **conversations are the funnel** — and they are not a stopgap. A twelve-year-old's school is chosen in a conversation, not in a room. Twenty of these before 14 January beats three information nights.

Each call has three jobs, in this order: find out whether this child is a fit, give the parent language to describe Soundline to the person who isn't on the phone, and end with a date.

---

## The frame

You already have the right structure and it is on your own homepage. A sounding line finds what is underneath before you set a course. **Run the call the way you run the school: depth first, direction second.** Pitch before you have sounded and you are selling to a parent whose actual problem you don't know.

Two rules hold the whole thing up:

- **Don't sell Soundline in the first ten minutes.** You lose nothing by waiting, and you learn what to say.
- **They should talk more than you.** If you talked more than half, it was a worse call and you have a worse record of it.

---

## The twenty minutes

### 0 · Before you dial — 2 min
Survey answers, grade, district, how they found you. Write one sentence: *what you think their problem is.* You are testing that guess, not confirming it.

### 1 · Frame it — 60 seconds

> "I've got about twenty minutes, and I'm not going to pitch you. I want to understand your kid, and then I'll tell you straight whether I think Soundline is a fit. Sometimes it isn't."

That last clause does more work than anything else you will say. A founder who will say no is a founder worth believing.

### 2 · The sounding — 8 to 10 min
Ask, then stop talking.

- "Tell me about [name]. Not school — what are they like?"
- "What's school like for them right now?" — *then say nothing for five seconds*
- "When did you last see them really lit up by something?"
- **"What made you pick up the phone?"** ← the highest-value question on the list. Whatever they say next is what you are actually selling against.
- "What would have to be true in June for you to say this year went well?"
- **"Who else is in this decision?"** ← never skip. The person on the phone is rarely the only decider, and the one who isn't on the phone is usually the one who says no.

Then ask their objection before they do:

- "What's your biggest worry about a school that doesn't exist yet?"

### 3 · Say it back — 2 min
Repeat their problem in their own words. Do not move on until they say *yes, exactly.* This is where the call is won. Everything after it is detail.

### 4 · The fit — 4 to 5 min
**Pick two things, not ten.** The mapping is below. A parent who came for one problem does not want the full tour; they want to hear their own sentence answered.

### 5 · Say the hard parts out loud — 2 min
The building isn't signed. Tuition isn't published until the 12th. This is the first cohort, which means their child is a founder and also, honestly, a guinea pig.

**Naming these yourself is the strongest move available to you.** They are already thinking all three, and every other school would dodge. Saying them first converts your biggest weakness into the reason they trust the rest.

### 6 · Close on a date — 2 min
Offer in descending order of commitment, and stop at the first yes:

1. **"Can I put you down for the 11th?"** — Wednesday 11 November, 7:00 pm, Port Jefferson Free Library. Tuition and registration follow the next morning, and people in the room hear the number first.
2. A second call with Adam or Izzy — for a parent whose worry is academic rather than social.
3. Coffee, if they are local and hesitant about a room.
4. The packet, plus a date you will call them back. *Only* with a date attached.

### 7 · The ask — 60 seconds

> "Who's one other family whose kid you'd want in the room with yours?"

Not "please share it." Specific, flattering and concrete, which is why it works. Ask on every call, including the ones that went badly.

### 8 · Within two hours
Three lines of email, with something from the call in the first line so it is obviously not a template. The date you agreed, and nothing else to decide.

## The fit mapping

| What they tell you | What you answer with | Where you send them |
|---|---|---|
| Bored, ahead, coasting | Grouped by skill, not age. Nobody waits once they've got it. | `day.html` |
| Anxious, lost in a big middle school | Eight to ten in a grade, one advisor for three years, nowhere to hide | `about.html` |
| Checked out, on screens | Two hours of hands-on practice; the only screen in the morning is the guide's | Screen-time FAQ on `join.html` |
| Bright, and nobody has noticed | A defence every six weeks, and a folder of work that travels | `vision.html` |
| "Will this count for high school?" | Ninth grade in year two, then a grade a year; NY's diploma is built on evidence | High-school FAQ on `join.html` |
| We homeschool | The Collaborative — three days, **by the term, not the year** | `collaborative.html` |
| It's the money | The number comes on the 12th; the federal scholarship starts in January | `cost.html` |

**For a homeschool family the close is different and easier.** They are already out of the system, so there is no leap to argue them across — and *by the term* is the whole pitch. Say it early. It is the only thing on offer locally that lets a cautious family try a first-year school without betting a year on it.

---

## What not to do

- **Don't discount.** You have not published a price. There is nothing to discount from, and offering money before a number exists tells them the number is soft.
- **Don't say "we're still working that out"** without a date attached. "The 12th" is an answer. "Soon" is a leak.
- **Don't end without a next step.** "I'll send you the packet" is not a next step.
- **Don't fill the silence.** Count to five. The sentence after a parent's pause is the true one.

---

## Keep the record

One line per call, in a sheet, same day:

```
Date · Parent · Child + grade · District · Found us via
Their words for the problem (verbatim, not paraphrased)
Their objection
Next step + date agreed
Referral given
```

Verbatim matters. Across ten calls the repeated phrases are your ad copy, your 11 November script, and the answer to which of the eleven FAQ answers on `join.html` is actually carrying the weight. Ten of these will reshape the channel mix in §4 of `PLAN-35.md` more than any benchmark in it.
