# Soundline Should Court CSF, ACE and AFC Now

Four non-sectarian scholarship granting organizations could plausibly be on New York's first §25F list and take a brand-new secular independent middle school in Miller Place: **Children's Scholarship Fund (CSF)**, headquartered in Manhattan and already selling "SGO services" to private schools; **ACE Scholarships**, which announced on 9 October 2026 that it will operate in all 50 states and maintains a New York tracking page; the **AFC Scholarship Fund**, a national SGO launched in January 2026 on the Odyssey platform that explicitly targets states without existing choice infrastructure; and **American GOAL**, Georgia GOAL's federal arm, which names "independent schools" among its partners. Every organization that is visibly preparing on Long Island or in New York City (Tomorrow's Hope Foundation for the Rockville Centre diocese, Inner-City Scholarship Fund, Futures in Education, the Opportunity Fund for Jewish Education, CEN SGO) is closed to a non-religious school by charter, so Soundline's partner will almost certainly be a national SGO that New York lists, not a local one. None of the four has announced New York operations since Governor Hochul's announcement because there is nothing yet to apply to: New York was still absent from the IRS participating-states list as of 22 October 2026, no state agency, form or intake deadline for SGOs has been named, and the only bill that would assign the job (S9494/A11639, to the Commissioner of Taxation and Finance) sits in committee. The recommended order is CSF first, this week, with a concrete ask that it act as Soundline's SGO through its SGO Services offering or a new Long Island program; ACE and AFC intake forms in parallel; American GOAL third. The deadline chain is fixed by federal rules: temporary regulations take effect and proposed-regulation comments close on 1 December 2026, New York's Form 15714 is due by 1 January 2027 (the same day gifts begin to count), and New York's certified SGO list is due 15 February 2027, after which no organization in the state qualifies for 2027 if the list is missed. Soundline's own critical path is a NYSED BEDS code effective 1 July 2027, which requires a certificate of occupancy and a Brookhaven fire-safety report, so the spring of 2027 is the BEDS filing window. The dominant risks are a late or thin New York list and the unresolved question of whether donors may designate a school; litigation is not currently a risk.

Contact details are recorded as found and must be re-verified before outreach. Statements marked *(inference)* are the research team's conclusions, not reported facts.

## CSF leads a four-name shortlist; every Long Island fund is closed to a secular school

The ranking below weighs three things: whether the SGO is non-sectarian in policy, whether it has signalled that it will seek listing in New York, and how lightweight its documented school-onboarding process is. CSF ranks first because it is the only candidate that is both New York-domiciled and explicitly offering to *be* the SGO for a private school. Its legal name is "The Childrens Scholarship Fund," EIN 13-4002189 ([GuideStar](https://www.guidestar.org/profile/13-4002189)), and its SGO Services page describes two models: CSF advises an organization running its own SGO, or CSF acts as the SGO "handling administration and compliance," with eligible partners including "private schools, dioceses, school networks, scholarship organizations, and other nonprofits" ([CSF SGO Services](https://scholarshipfund.org/sgoservices/), search extract). CSF has hired Lesley Searcy as Vice President and Head of Federal Scholarship Tax Credit ([CSF announcement](https://scholarshipfund.org/lesley-searcy-joins-csf-as-vice-president-head-of-federal-scholarship-tax-credit/)), its president Darla Romfo issued a statement on the proposed regulations on 1 October 2026 and has lobbied New York to opt in ([CSF News](https://scholarshipfund.org/news/); [The Hill op-ed](https://thehill.com/opinion/education/5955246-empowering-parents-education-choices/)), and its national policy is that "a scholarship can go to any legally operating private school," whether "parochial, denominational, independent, or a home school" ([CSF FAQ](https://scholarshipfund.org/apply/faq/), search extract). The caveat that shapes the ask: CSF New York's own program covers New York City plus seven Hudson Valley counties, uses a closed school list "designated by our funding partners," and routes Nassau and Suffolk through Tomorrow's Hope Foundation, which is Catholic-only ([CSF Apply](https://scholarshipfund.org/apply/), search extract). Soundline should therefore not ask to join CSF New York's list; it should ask CSF to serve as Soundline's SGO under SGO Services or to stand up a Long Island non-sectarian program, and it should raise the consortium idea (several Long Island independent schools under one CSF-administered program) because the 10-student, more-than-one-school rule makes a multi-school program more attractive to CSF than a single-school one *(inference)*.

ACE Scholarships ranks second on momentum. The Colorado Sun reported on 9 October 2026 that ACE "is expanding to all 50 states" under the federal credit, that its president said the organization is "prepared and ready to fundraise in all 50 states," and that it will serve charter and homeschool students as well as private-school students ([Colorado Sun](https://coloradosun.com/2026/10/09/colorado-federal-tax-credit-scholarship-ace-scholarships/), search extract). ACE already runs a page titled "Education Freedom Tax Credit in New York" that tracks the fact that New York "is not currently listed by the IRS as having made an advance election for 2027" ([ACE New York page](https://www.acescholarships.org/states/new-york/), search extract), and its track record is **121,000+ scholarships worth $395 million across 1,100+ partner schools in 13 states** ([ACE FTCS page](https://www.acescholarships.org/ftcs)). Its school onboarding is the lightest documented anywhere: email schools@acescholarships.org with the school's name, website, state, main contact, phone and grades served ([ACE help center](https://help.acescholarships.org/hc/en-us/articles/20390926669595-How-do-I-become-an-ACE-partner-school)). Two constraints matter for Soundline. ACE works only with "physical K-12 private schools" and says "the number of schools we can partner with in each state is dependent on the funds available" (same source), and its existing awards are modest, historically up to 50% of tuition with a $3,000 cap for K-8 ([ACE how to apply](https://help.acescholarships.org/hc/en-us/articles/21734116101275-How-to-Apply-A-Step-by-Step-Guide-on-Applying-for-an-ACE-Scholarship)); one partner school already advertises an "ACE Federal Tax Credit Scholarship, new for Spring 2027" for families under $270,000 ([SMAC Academy](https://academy.smac.edu/en/ace-scholarships-56944), search extract). The AFC Scholarship Fund ranks third because it is purpose-built for exactly New York's situation but has no New York footprint yet. The American Federation for Children announced it on 28 January 2026 as "a national scholarship granting organization" with "over $10 million" secured and Odyssey as platform ([AFC launch](https://www.federationforchildren.org/afc-announces-launch-of-affiliated-national-sgo-afc-scholarship-fund/)); its president is Sean Clifford ([AFC hires Clifford](https://www.federationforchildren.org/afc-hires-sean-clifford-as-president-of-afc-scholarship-fund/)); its model sends funds to "the educational program that fits their family — not to specific institutions" ([AFC SGO explainer](https://afcscholarshipfund.org/articles/what-is-a-scholarship-granting-organization-sgo/)); and its donor-conversion work targets "particularly … states that lack existing school choice infrastructure" ([Step Up, Step Further on AFC](https://stepupstepfurther.org/afc-partners-with-step-up-step-further/)), which describes New York, where no state scholarship credit exists ([Bonadio](https://www.bonadio.com/?p=5290)). ## New York has announced but not filed, and nobody knows which agency will run the list

The federal mechanics are settled and confirmed. A state elects by filing Form 15714, which may be filed any time "on or after January 1, 2026, and before the final date on which the State is permitted to submit the State SGO list," and the consequence of missing the list deadline is categorical: "no organization in that State would qualify as an SGO under § 25F for calendar year 2027" ([Rev. Proc. 2026-6 §§3.02, 4.01](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-26-06.pdf), IRS primary). The election must be made "by the Governor of the State or by such other individual, agency, or entity as is designated under State law," and a non-governor signer of Form 15714 "must include … the enacted statutory and/or regulatory provisions" conferring that authority ([Notice 2025-70 §2.04(2)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf); [Form 15714](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f15714.pdf), IRS primary). The state must independently verify every listed SGO, since "reliance by a covered State on self-certifications by SGOs would not be sufficient," and "the State list must include all organizations located in the State that have requested to be designated as an SGO and that meet the § 25F(c)(5) statutory requirements" ([Notice 2025-70 §§3.02–3.03](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf), IRS primary). The temporary regulations set the first-year dates at 1 January 2027 for the election and **15 February 2027** for the list ([eftccredit T.D. 10057 summary](https://eftccredit.com/documents/treasury-temporary-regulations-td-10057); [PKF O'Connor Davies](https://www.pkfod.com/insights/new-york-will-officially-opt-into-the-federal-scholarship-tax-credit-program/), search extracts). A Treasury official put the state's discretion bluntly: "states cannot pick and choose the SGOs they like" ([Stateline](https://stateline.org/2026/10/01/feds-provide-guidance-on-new-federal-k-12-scholarship-tax-credit-program/), search extract), and IR-2026-117 adds that states "may not impose SGO operating requirements stricter than section 25F, including limits based on school type" ([IR-2026-117](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/the-trump-administration-advances-the-establishment-of-americas-first-nationwide-school-choice-program), IRS primary). The practical meaning for Soundline is that New York cannot exclude a secular independent school or a national SGO that meets the statute; the only thing that can exclude Soundline is an SGO's own self-imposed focus *(inference)*.

New York's status is the weak link. Hochul announced on 9 October 2026 that New York "will participate," becoming only the second Democratic governor to opt in, after reviewing the October rules for a "poison pill" ([Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2026/10/09/ny-gov-hochul-opts-into-federal-scholarship-tax-credit/); [DFER](https://dfer.org/2026/10/09/hochul-opts-new-york-into-fstc/), search extracts). But the IRS participating-states page, current as of 22 October 2026, lists 30 states and **New York is not among them** ([IRS participating states](https://www.irs.gov/government-entities/federal-state-local-governments/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-fstc-participating-states), IRS primary), and EdChoice's institute tables New York as "Announced, not yet on the IRS list" ([EFI](https://www.efinstitute.org/2026/10/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-how-to-choose-sgo/), search extract). Whether New York filed between 22 and 29 October is unknown; the IRS page should be checked weekly. Which agency will run SGO intake is genuinely unknown: New York has no enacting statute, targeted searches for any role of the Division of the Budget, Taxation and Finance, NYSED or the Executive Chamber returned nothing, and the only bill on the subject, S9494 (Sen. Chan, co-sponsor Sen. Mattera) with companion A11639, would amend Tax Law §171 to make the Commissioner of Taxation and Finance submit and publish the list; S9494 is in the Senate Investigations and Government Operations committee and A11639 is in Assembly Ways and Means ([S9494](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S9494); [A11639](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/A11639), search extracts). With no designating statute, the governor herself is the only person who can sign both Form 15714 and the February certification, so the likeliest shape is an Executive Chamber-run checklist modelled on the Notice 2025-70 §3.03 certification, with Taxation and Finance or the Budget Division doing the verification *(inference)*. Texas, the closest analogue among opted-in states, elected by gubernatorial Form 15714 with "no state application process published yet" ([eftccredit Texas](https://eftccredit.com/states/texas), search extract, unverified), and as of late July only one of thirty opted-in states had sent Treasury a list ([AFC Scholarship Fund](https://afcscholarshipfund.org/articles/thirty-states-said-yes-only-one-has-sent-treasury-a-list/), search extract). Soundline should expect New York's intake to open with little notice and a short window, and should make sure its partner SGO has its certification package (EIN, EO Business Master File listing, segregated-account evidence, bylaws requiring §25F compliance, award and income-verification policies) ready to file within days *(inference)*.

Two points make the plan feasible for a school that will have zero students on 15 February 2027. First, §25F regulates the SGO, not the school: the IRS lists no school-level certification among what states must verify ([IRS FSTC page](https://www.irs.gov/government-entities/federal-state-local-governments/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-fstc), IRS primary), the Congressional Research Service notes the program places no "requirements on private schools that enroll scholarship recipients" ([CRS R48724](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48724), search extract), and Venable concludes participation "will not subject independent schools to federal compliance obligations as a recipient of federal financial assistance" ([Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/08/federal-school-choice-program-2025-what-it-means), search extract). Second, the proposed regulations measure the 90% spending test "as of the last day of the taxable year following the taxable year in which such income is received," counted when paid ([Federal Register REG-117199-25](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/02/2026-20277/federal-scholarship-tax-credit), search extract), so an SGO can hold gifts received in early 2027 and disburse them to Soundline families across the 2027-28 school year without any compliance pressure *(inference)*. The one thing a school cannot do is receive money before it exists as a school: qualified expenses must be "incurred at, required by, or provided by a public, private, or religious school" under §530(b)(3)(A) ([Notice 2025-70 §2.03(2)(c)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf), IRS primary), and "school" means one that provides K-12 education "as determined under State law" ([Scholarship Tax Credits](https://www.scholarshiptaxcredits.org/section-25f-education-freedom-tax-credit/), search extract). In New York the practical proxy for that is the BEDS code, which is why it sits on the critical path *(inference)*.

If the four national candidates all stall, the consortium route exists but is a 2028 project. A Long Island independent-school SGO would be legally simple, since the statute requires only "10 or more students who do not all attend the same school" ([Notice 2025-70 §2.03(2)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf), IRS primary), and both Venable and PKF O'Connor Davies suggest independent schools "band together and create an independent SGO" ([PKF](https://www.pkfod.com/insights/new-federal-tax-credit-for-scholarship-granting-organizations-will-it-impact-your-school/); [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/08/federal-school-choice-program-2025-what-it-means), search extracts). But a full Form 1023 is currently taking roughly six months ([Form1023.org](https://form1023.org/how-long-the-irs-takes-to-process-form-1023-for-501c3), search extract), New York's Charities Bureau registration must precede any solicitation ([NY AG Charities](https://ag.ny.gov/resources/government-organizations/charities-nonprofits-fundraisers/charities-registration)), and inclusion on the 2027 list would depend on New York choosing to list a pending applicant formed before 1 January 2027 ([Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/news/scholarship-groups-should-prepare-for-compliance-now-tax-pro-says/); [eftccredit](https://eftccredit.com/learn/how-to-start-an-sgo), search extracts). The faster variant is to persuade an existing Long Island 501(c)(3) public charity to open a segregated §25F account and serve as the consortium's SGO, which sidesteps the 1023 bottleneck *(inference)*; no such organization has surfaced.

## The mechanics that will shape the deal: designated giving, fees, instalments and a generous income ceiling

The question that most affects Soundline's fundraising is whether donors can direct §25F gifts toward Soundline. The statute bars earmarking only "on behalf of any particular student" ([Notice 2025-70 §2.03(2)(e)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf), IRS primary), and the temporary regulations require multistate SGOs to let donors designate a *state* ([Notice 2025-70 §3.03(5)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf), IRS primary). School-level designation is simply not addressed: Fisher Phillips says "the Regulations do not address whether school-specific donor designations are permitted" ([Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/proposed-federal-school-choice-regulations-what-private-schools-need-to-know)), Baker & Hostetler reads the answer as "clearly yes" ([Baker & Hostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/tax-credit-for-donations-to-scholarship-granting-organizations/)), and a compliance guide warns against "allocating scholarship funds by school in proportion to donations from that school's community" ([sgoguide](https://sgoguide.com/blog/compliant-scholarship-award-process)) (all search extracts). Practice in state programs is permissive: Indiana law requires that donors be allowed to designate a school while barring SGOs from serving only one school ([Indiana DOE FAQ](https://secure.in.gov/tos/inesa/files/SGO-FAQ-for-Parents-and-Donors.pdf)), Ohio's OCEN sends "all funds designated for a school, less the nine percent administrative fee" to scholarships at that school ([Worthington Christian](https://www.worthingtonchristian.com/?p=104065)), and the Jewish community's SGO intends to let donors earmark "for specific school or for a specific community" ([Teach Coalition](https://teachcoalition.org/federalscholarships/)) (search extracts). The defensible position for 2027 is that a New York SGO may let a donor express a preference for Soundline provided the SGO retains documented discretion over every award, no student is named, and the SGO as a whole serves 10+ students at more than one school; Soundline should not tell donors a gift "goes to Soundline" until its partner's counsel signs off *(inference)*. This matters commercially because a designated-school fund is what makes a zero-student school fundable: Soundline's own community gives in calendar 2027, the SGO holds the balance less its fee, and awards flow to enrolled, income-verified Soundline families for 2027-28 *(inference)*.

On money, plan for the SGO to keep 5–10% and to pay in instalments. The statute itself requires at least 90% of income on scholarships ([Notice 2025-70 §2.03(2)(b)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf), IRS primary); Indiana caps administration at 10% ([Ind. Code §20-51-3-3](https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/2015/title-20/article-51/chapter-3/)), Pennsylvania moved to 90/10 on 1 July 2024 with organizations averaging 93.8% payout ([Commonwealth Foundation](https://commonwealthfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Education-Tax-Credit-Scholarships-paper-Sept2024.pdf)), and OCEN charges 9% ([Lake Center Christian School](https://www.lccs.com/support-lccs/sgo/)) (search extracts). Florida's Step Up pays schools quarterly "once enrollment is verified and the student is submitted for payment," and only after the parent approves in the portal ([Step Up schedule](https://go.stepupforstudents.org/hubfs/SUFS_2025_PaymentDistroSchedule_.pdf)); schools must supply "proof of admission, the school's and student's fee schedules" ([Step Up handbook](https://www.stepupforstudents.org/?p=67789)); Georgia GOAL uses a three-party agreement with prorated refunds on withdrawal ([Mount Paran handbook](https://handbooks.mtparanschool.com/books/2026-2027-mount-paran-christian-school-parent-and-student-handbook/page/goal-student-scholarship-procedures)) (search extracts). One precedent deserves attention for a school with no operating history: Florida requires a private school accepting scholarship students to have operated "for at least 3 school years or" to post "a surety bond or letter of credit for the amount equal to the scholarship funds for any quarter" ([Fla. Stat. 1002.421](https://www.flsenate.gov/laws/statutes/2025/1002.421)). That is a Florida statute, not a federal rule, but a cautious SGO may ask Soundline for a bond, letter of credit or escrow terms before paying a quarter's tuition in advance *(inference)*.

The income ceiling will exclude almost nobody in Miller Place. An eligible student's household income "for the calendar year prior to the date of the application" may not exceed "300 percent of the area median gross income (as such term is used in § 42)" ([Notice 2025-70 §4.05(3)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf), IRS primary), measured "in a manner generally consistent with Section 8 determinations, including adjustments for family size," with the IRS planning to publish the limits by area and family size ([Federal Register REG-117199-25](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/02/2026-20277/federal-scholarship-tax-credit), search extract). Verification may rely on pay stubs, tax returns, W-2s or commercial data, with categorical eligibility through SNAP, TANF, WIC, Section 8 or SSI ([Treasury preview](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/136/Preview-of-Forthcoming-Guidance.pdf), search extract). The Town of Brookhaven's 2026 HUD table puts the 80% limit for a one-person household at $92,050 ([Brookhaven income guidelines](https://www.brookhavenny.gov/367/View-Income-Guidelines)), which implies a four-person Nassau-Suffolk median family income somewhere around $160,000 and a 300% ceiling well above $300,000 for a family of four; that arithmetic is the research team's, the HUD four-person figure was not retrieved, and the official 2027 §25F table does not yet exist *(inference)*. The credit itself is **$1,700 per taxpayer, $3,400 for joint filers**, nonrefundable, with a five-year carryforward, reduced by any state credit and not deductible under §170 ([IRS FSTC page](https://www.irs.gov/government-entities/federal-state-local-governments/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-fstc); [Notice 2025-70 §2.02](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf), IRS primary), and donors anywhere in the country may give to a New York-listed SGO ([IR-2026-117](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/the-trump-administration-advances-the-establishment-of-americas-first-nationwide-school-choice-program), IRS primary), which means Soundline's donor pool is not limited to Long Island.

## A late or thin New York list is the real threat, not a lawsuit

Opposition is loud and institutional but has no legal lever. NYSUT's president called the program "President Trump's private-school voucher program" and said "this fight is far from over"; UFT's Michael Mulgrew called the opt-in "a failure of leadership"; Senate Majority Leader Stewart-Cousins and Speaker Heastie both urged opting out; the NYC Public Advocate and the NAACP New York State Conference opposed it ([NY State of Politics](https://nystateofpolitics.com/state-of-politics/new-york/ny-state-of-politics/2026/10/12/new-york-federal-education-tax-credit); [FingerLakes1](https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2026/10/09/new-york-legislative-leaders-press-hochul-to-reject-federal-scholarship-tax-credit/); [NYC Public Advocate](https://advocate.nyc.gov/press/nyc-public-advocate-and-naacp-of-ny-oppose-governors-optin-to-federal-scholarship-tax-credit), search extracts). Yet no lawsuit against §25F or New York's opt-in was found, no opt-out bill exists, and the Legislature has no formal role because the election is a gubernatorial act under federal law ([Notice 2025-70 §2.04(2)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf), IRS primary). The reversal mechanism is procedural: the election is annual ([Form 15714 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f15714.pdf), IRS primary), and a state that files Form 15714 can effectively withdraw by not filing its list, exactly the pressure the Virginia Education Association is applying to Governor Spanberger ([eftccredit Virginia](https://eftccredit.com/news/virginia-2027-hinges-on-sgo-list-vea-urges-spanberger-not-to-file-october-2026), search extract). The plausible bad scenario for Soundline is not a reversal but an administration that files on time and then produces a thin or late February list because no agency owns the work *(inference)*.

Three further risks are federal. The substantive rules remain proposed, with comments due 1 December and a hearing 15 December 2026, so the 90% safe harbor, verification rules and anything Treasury says about school designation could shift after Soundline and its partner have designed a program ([Federal Register REG-117199-25](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/02/2026-20277/federal-scholarship-tax-credit), search extract); the detailed guidance on what counts as an eligible expense is a separate workstream with no timeline ([Chalkbeat Detroit](https://www.chalkbeat.org/detroit/2026/10/27/michigan-governor-gretchen-whitmer-raises-questions-about-tax-credit-program/), search extract); and five Democratic governors and House Ways and Means Democrats have written to Treasury seeking changes, unanswered as of 27 October ([Education Week](https://www.edweek.org/policy-politics/democratic-governors-have-questions-about-the-federal-school-choice-program/2026/10), search extract). A timing wrinkle deserves counsel's attention: the credit starts 1 January 2027 but the list is not due until 15 February, and Notice 2025-70 protects donors only where the organization "was on the list at the time of the gift" ([Notice 2025-70 §3.02](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-70.pdf), IRS primary). Whether a gift made in January to an SGO listed in February relates back is not settled in any document read; Soundline should hold its donors until the list is public rather than test the question *(inference)*.

The unknowns to track are concrete. Nobody has identified the New York agency, form, portal or internal deadline for SGOs to request listing; no New York SGO list exists; no SGO has published a policy on schools that are not yet operating or a fee schedule for the federal program; whether ORISS will issue a BEDS code to a school with no enrolled students at filing is undocumented ([NYSED BEDS info](https://www.nysed.gov/nonpublic-schools/basic-education-data-system-beds-code-information), search extract); the exact mechanics of a mid-year list addition could not be read; and the Governor's own 9 October statement could not be retrieved, so its precise wording and whether any executive memorandum accompanied it are unverified. Each of these is a question Soundline can ask directly, of ORISS, of the Executive Chamber, and of each candidate SGO, and the answers should be in hand before the 1 December target.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem: Soundline is not looking for a Long Island scholarship fund that will add a new school, because no such non-sectarian fund exists and the ones that do are Catholic or Jewish by charter. It is looking for a national SGO that will seek a place on a New York list that no agency has yet been assigned to build. That changes the posture from applicant to early customer. CSF, ACE, AFC and American GOAL are each assembling New York pipelines they cannot yet file, so a school that arrives in November with a complete dossier, a credible donor base and a willingness to anchor a multi-school Long Island program is offering them something, not asking a favour. The empty non-sectarian niche on Long Island is also leverage: whichever SGO lists first with an open-school policy will inherit every independent school on the Island, and Soundline can make that case to CSF in particular.

The second implication is that Soundline's fundraising story and its regulatory story are the same story. Because the only workable way to fund a school with no enrolment history is a designated-school fund whose legality is unresolved, the partner's willingness to run such a fund, and its counsel's reading of the earmarking rule, should be the decisive selection criterion, ahead of fee percentage or platform. Pressing that question in the 1 December comment window, and asking each candidate for a written position on it before 15 January, will do more to secure September 2027 scholarships than anything else on the calendar.
